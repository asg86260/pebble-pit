# Store pages of small games: what they do (2026-10-09)

Read for the 1.0 page. One line a page; quotes are a few words at most.
Review counts and awards seen on these pages are left out on purpose --
Pebble Pit has none to show, and the page should not pretend to.

## itch.io

- [1,000,000 shrimp](https://jontopielski.itch.io/1000000-shrimp) -- the whole description is about fifteen words: the goal, the one control, the fullscreen key. Play first, then two shots. 1-bit look, tagged `1-bit`.
- [plant daddy](https://overfull.itch.io/plantdaddy) -- one sentence of pitch (about 50 words in all), "name your own price", HTML5 plus Windows/mac/Linux downloads, three shots at the foot.
- [Shleep](https://bippinbits.itch.io/shleep) -- play area, then about 150 words with the controls written out (left-click / right-click), credits, and two GIFs rather than stills.
- [outline](https://managore.itch.io/outline) -- a one-bit game sold on four looping GIFs; pay what you want; a short FAQ under the pitch.
- [Hidden Folks](https://adriaan.itch.io/hidden-folks) -- black-and-white hand drawing; pitch first, then a bullet list of countable facts (areas, targets, sounds), then price and platforms.

## Steam

- [Nodebuster](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3107330/Nodebuster/) -- one-sentence short description that says what it is ("short, experimental incremental"), three one-line paragraphs, no headings.
- [(the) Gnorp Apologue](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1473350/the_Gnorp_Apologue/) -- four short bold headings, the first being the verb you do ("Hit the rock"), then what you buy, then the strategy.
- [Cookie Clicker](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1454400/Cookie_Clicker/) -- short description in under ten words, then six feature bullets, each a countable fact.
- [Unpacking](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1135690/Unpacking/) -- about 50 words of description, five bullets, system requirements split by OS.
- [Increlution](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1593350/Increlution/) -- each section heading is a mechanic; one heading is the honest-numbers promise ("Understandable numbers"); a free first chapter.
- [Townscaper](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1291340/Townscaper/) -- opens by saying what it is *not* (no goal), then the one verb; short.
- [Melvor Idle](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1267910/Melvor_Idle/) -- the long end: headings, bullet lists and GIFs per system. Works for a huge game; too much for this one.
- [Universal Paperclips](https://www.decisionproblem.com/paperclips/) -- the landing page is a title and two links; the game is the pitch.

## What to take

1. **Short beats long.** The best-rated small pages pitch in one or two sentences and let the play button or a GIF do the rest.
2. **Play first on itch.** The embed sits above the text; the text is for the person who scrolled past it.
3. **Motion sells a watching game.** The one-bit and idle pages that show GIFs show the thing moving; for a game whose point is bodies walking, the hero slot should be a loop, not a still.
4. **Bullets are facts, not adjectives.** The good lists are countable or checkable ("mouse only", "pay what you want"); none of the small pages lean on superlatives.
5. **Say the deliberate choices up front.** Controls, where the save lives, what it is not -- written on the page, so they are the description and not the complaint (`docs/itch-page.md` already does this).
6. **Metadata is a table.** itch's "More information" block (status, platforms, inputs, session length, accessibility) carries what Steam puts in system requirements; keep the prose free of it.
7. **No spoilers is normal.** None of these pages shows its late game; they sell the first ten minutes and the feel.
