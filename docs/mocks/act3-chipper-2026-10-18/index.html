Act 3: working the chipper by hand, in the world

A playable mock drawn from plain cells on the game's grid (P = 6, the game's eleven greys, a body the yard's 18 px square with a 2 px rim), not the game. The station is take B, the jaws, as built in src/render/after/stations.js (chipper), drawn facing right, at zoom 1 (a cell is 6 px, a body 3 cells). Each take is drawn twice: the 3x view to play on, and the 1x picture beside it, which plays too. Mouse and touch both work.

The feeder. Tick post a feeder and a body walks in from the left. It loads at the pile, carries the log on its head, places it in the jaws and works the control. Halfway through each log it lets go for a second, which shows the jaws stopping and starting again. It works the jaws at the same steady speed as the hand, but the walking, the pauses and the rest make it about 9 s a log against about 6 s for a player. While it is posted, the hand cannot use the station.

Two steps. 1: grab a log off the pile and drop it in the jaws (a drop anywhere else slides it back to the pile). 2: hold the control; the jaws bite at a steady speed until the log is gone, stop when you let go, and go on when you hold again. One log at a time. A carrier walks logs onto the pile from the left edge, so nothing appears from nowhere.

Every word on this page is a placeholder (marked like this). The canvases draw no words.

Dark is ink #e0e0e0 on paper #1f1f1f, each grey mapped by its lightness, not inverted.

Pick: (b) the lever. Its pressed pose is the biggest change of the three (a plumb rod laid level, its knob moving five cells), so nobody wonders whether it is down. It is the press's bar again, a rod pulled down at the body's hand, which ties the chipper into the camp's family. The worker reads at a glance: a body with a lever laid by it. (a) is the clearest thing to press with a mouse but the plainest to look at, and its pressed state is a 3 px drop. (c) is the best worker (the body stands on it, and steps off to rest) and the worst for the hand: a pedal is a foot's control, thin at 1x, and nobody clicks a floor.

What the mock cannot show

  • The camp around it: no crowd, no other stations, no camera. The station has the page to itself.
  • The card, the sound and the coin the sawdust becomes. The heap only grows; nobody carries it off.
  • The game's frame timing. Here time is the browser's own clock.
