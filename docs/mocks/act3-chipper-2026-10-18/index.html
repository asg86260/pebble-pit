Each take is drawn twice: the 3x view to play on, and the 1x picture beside it, which plays too. Mouse and touch both work.

Dark is ink #e0e0e0 on paper #1f1f1f, each grey mapped by its lightness, not inverted.

the chosen control: the arm

The vote. The owner kept the log dragging and picked the lever, worked like a slot machine's arm: click the knob, drag it down and hold it there.

The arm. A long arm with a ball knob, on a pivot box at the jaws' foot. It moves in three held poses, never at an odd angle: plumb (let go), 45 degrees, and level (pulled). The knob is a solid ball with a glint. The box is solid ink with a white ring round its pin, so it does not read as a body.

Using it. 1: drag a log off the pile into the jaws, exactly as before. 2: click the knob and drag it down (straight down or down along its arc; only the drop counts). A partial pull shows the arm at 45 degrees and does nothing. At the bottom, holding, the jaws bite at a steady speed, about 0.7 s a bite and six bites a log. Let go and the arm springs back up a pose at a time; the jaws finish the bite they are in, lift open and stop, mid-log. Pull and hold again to go on.

The cut. Each bite, the top jaw comes down until its tooth tips (white-edged, so they show against the wood) land on the log. A white kerf opens across the log at the block's mouth, that cell breaks into grey crumbs while the tooth over it goes on down through it, and as the jaw lifts the log is pushed in a cell. The chips come out of the chute a moment later. The log shortens only on the bite that cut it.

The readout is on the machine now: a gauge on the face of the back block, filling a row from the bottom with each bite and fading away when the log is done.

The feeder. Tick post a feeder. It loads at the pile, carries the log on its head, places it in the jaws, then hops up, grabs the knob and rides the arm down with its weight, a pose at a time, and stays there holding it while the jaws bite. Halfway through each log it lets go to rest for a second (the arm springs up and the jaws stop), then hops up and pulls again.

Cues. The ghost hand first shows drag a log here (pile to jaws, dotted); once a log is in, it shows pull down and hold: it takes the knob, a faint ghost arm steps down to level, and three dots light while it holds. Each fades for good after three goes.

What the mock cannot show

  • The camp around it: no crowd, no other stations, no camera. The station has the page to itself.
  • The card, the sound and the coin the sawdust becomes. The heap only grows; nobody carries it off.
  • The game's frame timing. Here time is the browser's own clock.
  • A body with arms. The yard's bodies are squares, so the feeder "holds" the knob by overlapping it; the ride down is the hop and the weight.

      The last round: three takes on the control

      The feeder. Tick post a feeder and a body walks in from the left. It loads at the pile, carries the log on its head, places it in the jaws and works the control. Halfway through each log it lets go for a second, which shows the jaws stopping and starting again. It works the jaws at the same steady speed as the hand, but the walking, the pauses and the rest make it about 9 s a log against about 6 s for a player. While it is posted, the hand cannot use the station.

      Two steps. 1: grab a log off the pile and drop it in the jaws (a drop anywhere else slides it back to the pile). 2: hold the control; the jaws bite at a steady speed until the log is gone, stop when you let go, and go on when you hold again. One log at a time. A carrier walks logs onto the pile from the left edge, so nothing appears from nowhere.

      The pile moved a cell left this round (to make room for the arm's level pose); these takes are otherwise as voted on.
