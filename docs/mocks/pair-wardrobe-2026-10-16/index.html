The two of you: what they wear

Mock, not the game. Drawn from plain cells the way the last mock (docs/mocks/pair-identity-2026-10-16) was: a body is the 3 x 3 cell square (WORKER, 18 world pixels) with a 2-pixel black rim and a white middle; the station hats are the five in HATS (src/sprites.js), in black as the game draws them. One cell is 6 world pixels. "Game scale" is one world pixel to one screen pixel, as on an 800-wide window. The canvases loop on held frames (12 a second).

Follows the owner's note on the last mock: choosing what they wear, yes; ignore the hats on the main squares and show the chosen headwear, or overlay them; expand the clothes.

Part 1: the station hat on the two of them

One card per station hat. In each: the plain crew body in that hat for reference, then four chosen headwear, each shown twice: a the chosen thing replaces the station hat, b the two drawn as one hat, the station hat's one telling detail kept (the helmet's bar, the lamp a cell proud at the front, the sun hat's brim, the wizard's point, the cap's block). Top row 3x, bottom row game scale.

Verdict on the hats

  • Replace (a) is the rule. The game already says the job by where a body stands (the comment on drawBody: "no job carries a mark of its own"), so the pair lose nothing they need, and their headwear stays one constant shape across every station, the deep and act 3, which is what makes them findable.
  • Overlay reads well on two hats: the sun hat and the point. A brim under a bow, a top hat or a crown is a sun hat with a ribbon or a crown on it, still farm at game scale. The point grows out of tall things (top hat, flower) well.
  • Overlay half-reads on the lamp. The one cell proud at the front reads as a lamp on the top hat (it sits on the hat's face) and nowhere else; on the bow and the crown it is a stub.
  • Overlay does not read on the helmet and the cap. Both are a black bar; merged, either one is "the headwear on a stripe", the two are the same picture a row apart, and the top hat swallows the cap whole. Merging there is really stacking.
  • Overlay also makes the pair the tallest bodies in the yard (point + flower is five cells on a three-cell body), which competes with loads carried over the head.
  • If the owner wants (b) anyway, the cheap shape is: sun hat and point merged by hand-drawn tables, the other three replaced.

Part 2: the wardrobe

Five slots that combine on one body. Each item drawn at 3x and at game scale beside it. The tiles walk and stop on a four-second loop so the things that move (scarves, capes, tails) show both poses. Item names are plain labels, not final copy.

Does the body have a face?

No. drawBody (src/render/crew.js) is one square: a black fill and a white fill inset by BODY_RIM (2). The face items below are therefore new marks on the square's white middle, which is 14 x 14 world pixels: less than three cells. They cannot sit on the 6-pixel cell grid, so they are drawn in 2-pixel units, the rim's own weight, front-biased toward the way the body faces. That breaks "everything on the P = 6 grid"; the same is true of the neck marks and the patterns. The head and back slots are whole cells.

Which slots survive game scale

  • Head: survives. It is the silhouette. Every head item is a different outline over the crowd. Weakest: ears, mohawk and tiara (one or two cells), and beanie, which is the cap with one cell more.
  • Back: survives, best in motion. The cape and scarf tails stream; the balloon and flag stand above the crowd and are the easiest things on the page to find. Clash: a carter's cart hangs off the same side, and the balloon and flag sit where speech marks go.
  • Pattern: survives as a tone. Every crew body is white, so a grey, black, striped or overalled square is found at once, even with no head item. Fine patterns (dots, diagonal) read only as "grey".
  • Neck: half. The scarves survive because their tails leave the square. On-body marks (bowtie, tie, necklace, collar, medal, bandana) are a dark smudge at game scale: you see "something on the front", not which thing.
  • Face: disappears in a crowd. At game scale a face is three or four dark pixels. Shades and glasses survive as a dark bar; freckles and blush (grey) vanish. Faces are for the close-ups: the picker, the house, act 3. Face and pattern fight: a face on stripes or dots is lost at any scale.

Part 3: the picker

A sheet in the game's menu style. Both squares large at the top; tap one (or the her / him tabs) to dress it. One row a slot with prev / next; a randomize. The strip under the squares is the two of them at game scale in a crowd, so the choice is made for findability, not only for the close-up. The controls work in this mock: click the arrows, or use the keyboard as a stand-in for a pad.

How it works

  • Desktop. Click a square or a tab to choose who; click the arrows. The row under the cursor is boxed so the keyboard and the mouse share one cursor.
  • Phone. The same sheet in the one sheet seat (as the settings sheet). Each row is two lines: its label, then a 44-pixel prev, the item's name, a 44-pixel next. Squares at 3x so both fit side by side; the crowd strip under them stays at game scale, which on a phone is smaller still. Swipe is not used: the yard already owns one-finger scroll.
  • Pad. Up / down moves the boxed row, left / right cycles the item, shoulders swap her and him, Y randomizes the one being dressed, A is done, B closes without keeping. Every row wraps around, "none" first.
  • Randomize dresses only the selected one, so a good outfit on the other is not lost. Held (or pressed twice) it could roll both; a call for the owner.
  • Where the sheet is opened from (new game, the settings row, earned items) is the last mock's Part 2 question, unchanged here.
  • Words marked like this are placeholders for the owner to word.

What this mock cannot show

  • The real zoom: the yard's zoom changes with the window and a phone draws smaller, so "game scale" here is one size of several. Faces and neck marks are worse on a phone than shown.
  • The rest of the yard: rocks, heaps, carts, loads carried over the head, dust and grains in the air, speech marks and dizzy stars. A load over the head hides head items; a cart hides back items.
  • How the game snaps a moving body: here a body slides a pixel at a time; the game rounds rims to device pixels at fractional zooms, and 2-pixel face marks at a fractional zoom will blur or drop a column. Not tested.
  • The deep: the black ground, its palette rules (no greys it does not hold), class kit on the fighters. Patterns that use grey may not be allowed there.
  • Under the rock in act 1: she is mostly buried, so only head items show, if any.
  • The boards and the roster, which draw the same square beside each count.
  • Hop, stoop, squat (the squashed two-row body) and carrying poses: every slot here assumes the square is square.
  • The sheet is drawn by hand to look like the game's, not with its stylesheet.
