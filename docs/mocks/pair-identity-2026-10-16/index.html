The two of you: telling them apart

Mock, not the game. Every picture here is drawn from plain cells on a 6-pixel grid with the game's rules copied by hand: a body is the 3 x 3 cell square with a 2-pixel black rim and a white middle, and the station hats are the five in HATS (src/sprites.js), sitting on the head. The canvases loop. Ten other crew walk in every yard strip; the two you are looking for are always there, both of them wearing a station hat, because that is the worst case.

Where the two of them are, act by act

  • Act 1, the rock. She is lodged in the ground under every rock (drawBuried, render/crew.js), sunk a cell at a time and mostly hidden by the rock's cells; he is the first digger, one of the crew. Once she is dug out she becomes an ordinary crew body (intro.js, S.crew++ and a fresh worker): nothing in the save says which body she is.
  • The snatch. The game takes the two crew bodies nearest the mouth (snatch.js, takeOff) and plays them as the pair. He is taken; she leaps in after him.
  • Act 2, the deep. She is the deep's first body: the fighter at station one, living in the first pod, on a black ground. He is in the serpent's belly, unseen, until the fourth defense breaks.
  • The finale. Both swim up, walk the whole yard at the head of two files of crew, and go into the house (finale.js); they live there for good (S.retired).
  • Act 3 (being designed). Two square souls floating up into the clouds.

So "always able to be seen" is not only a drawing. Today the pair are anonymous between the rescue and the snatch. Any option below needs the game to keep two named bodies across act 1 (her, and the digger as him) and give those two to the snatch, instead of whichever two stand nearest the mouth. That is a save-shape change (two uids on S).

Part 1: five ways to mark them

Each card: the pair close up at 3x walking; both under every station hat at 2x; the yard crowd at game scale (one world pixel to one screen pixel, as on an 800-pixel-wide window); the deep at game scale; and the act-3 souls at 2x.

Part 2: choosing what they wear

Three ways in, sketched as the game's sheets. Words marked like this are placeholder copy for the owner to word; the rest are plain labels. The eight drawings are the same in all three flows, and either of the two can wear any of them.

Eight things to wear (3x, on a body, and stacked on a helmet)

bow, top hat, crown, flower, sprout, ears, antenna, scarf. Seven sit on the head, so with a station hat on they stack on top of it (row two). The scarf hangs off the side and never meets a hat.

What this mock cannot show

  • The real zoom: the game's yard zoom changes with the window (a phone draws it smaller), so "game scale" here is one size of several.
  • The rest of the yard: rocks, heaps, carts, forklifts, dust in the air, falling grains, the crew's speech marks and dizzy stars, which compete with a floating mark (B) far more than ten plain bodies do.
  • Under the rock: she is mostly hidden by the rock's cells in act 1, so only what sticks out above the ground line (a tuft, an accessory on the head) can show there. Not drawn here.
  • The deep's real party: class kit (swords, staves, the mage's things) drawn on the fighters, the serpent over them, the deep's camera, which is wide and only pans.
  • How the game walks a body: here they slide a pixel at a time with held two-pose waves for the scarf and the bob; no hop, no stoop, no carrying pose.
  • The boards and the crew list, which draw the same square beside each count; whether the pair's mark shows there too is a separate call.
  • The menus are pictures of sheets, not the sheet's real stylesheet, and none of the controls work.
