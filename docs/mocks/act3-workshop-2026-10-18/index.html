The workshop: a roof for the shed, and nailing signs by hand

A playable mock drawn from plain cells on the game's grid (P = 6, the game's eleven greys, a body the game's 18-pixel square with a 2-pixel rim), not the game. The shed body is take B as built (wsB in src/render/after/stations.js: walls 6..24, the open front 8..22), with the same numbers; only the roof and what happens on the bench are new. The press is redrawn from the same file, working, as the bar the shed has to sit beside.

Every word on this page is a placeholder (marked like this, and the whole page is): station, job, roof and cue words are working names. The canvases draw no words, only counts. The owner writes the player's words.

Dark is ink #e0e0e0 on paper #1f1f1f, each grey mapped by its lightness (black to the ink, white to the paper, a grey to the same step between). It is not an inversion.

2. Nailing, playable, under roof

Tap a nail on the bench (mouse or a finger, on the 3x picture or the 1x one). The first tap on a nail sinks it halfway, the second drives it flush. A tap that lands on no nail, or on a nail already flush, does nothing. When all four are flush the carrier takes the sign out to the sign pile, fetches a blank from the blanks and lays it on the bench, and the next four nails go in.

tap each nail

How it reads

  1. The nails. The blank lies on the bench edge-on, twelve cells long and one deep, and the four nails stand up out of its top edge three cells apart, so each nail owns a three-cell column to aim at. A raised nail is a two-pixel shank eight pixels tall under a one-cell head: it stands well clear of the board. Half is the same nail with half the shank showing. Flush is the head alone, a two-pixel bump on the board's top edge. The three are told apart by height, which is what reads at 1x.
  2. The hammer. The joiner stands behind the bench on a slatted stand and walks along it a cell at a time to the nail tapped (nothing jumps), hammer raised. Then three held poses as built: up (handle plumb, head level over the shoulder), a grey smear for one step, and the strike (head plumb, standing on the nail's head, handle level back to the hand). The nail changes on the strike, not on the tap, so the click is the cause and the blow is the effect.
  3. Taps queue. Tap all four nails twice as fast as you like: each tap is a blow in the joiner's queue, done in order. A third tap on a nail is a miss (it would have nowhere to go).
  4. The pips are cut into the sign over the door: a ring for a raised nail, half-filled for half, solid for flush. They are the station's readout and need no words.
  5. The ghost hand points down at the next nail that still wants a tap, tapping in two held poses, and walks to the next nail a cell at a time. It shows for the first three signs and fades out over the fourth. Its words (tap each nail) are page text beside it, not drawn.
  6. The finished sign slides along the bench a cell a step onto the carrier's head, is walked out to the sign pile, lowered onto it a row a step and fades into the pile as the count ticks. The carrier walks on to the blanks, lifts the top one off, walks it back and slides it onto the bench; the four new nails fade in standing.
  7. The joiner on their own taps one blow about every second, left to right, once you have not tapped for a second and a half. You tap up to about three times as fast; the walk out and back is the same for both.

Round one (after the first shots)

    Round two (after fixing round one)

      What the mock cannot show

      • The real camp: the cloud floor, the stair, the build in bands, the glyph stack over the station and the pile-full mark. The crowd behind is the r2 mock's stand-in.
      • The hand's real box in after/place.js, or how a tap on a nail would compete with a tap on a body or a pile standing in front of the shed. Here the only thing tappable is a nail.
      • The game's sign: the camp's signs are drawn on a post (sign() in the r2 mock). Here the product stays a board edge-on, so the pile is a stack of boards with nail heads; whether a finished board should stand up into the post-sign before it is carried is open.
      • Who brings blanks to the blanks pile (the timber from the saw mill): the stack here counts down from a fixed number.
      • Whether a joiner standing behind the bench breaks anything in the game's members layer, which draws bodies on the floor. The stand behind the bench is new.
      • Sound, and what a finger feels on a phone at 1x: a nail's column is 18 px wide at the game's zoom.

      Open questions for the owner

      • Should the joiner keep tapping on their own while the player is tapping too (both hammers' worth of blows), or stop whenever the hand is on it, as here?
      • The carrier is a second body at the station. It could be the joiner stepping down instead (the player waits longer) or a carrier from the camp's pool.
      • Four nails a sign makes eight taps a sign. Is a sign worth eight taps against the other stations' gestures?
