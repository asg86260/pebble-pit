Act 3: the throne and the tithe stair, three takes

A mock drawn from plain shapes, not the game. God (face c, serene), his glare, halo and rays, the throne back and the sky crowd are simplified redraws of what is built. Each take replaces the tower and the dotted ledge. Stone and cloud use four tones only: paper, pale grey, mid grey and ink. God's light is the one exception, kept raw, so it stays the brightest thing on both pages. It cannot show the camera's pan or dive, parallax, the crowd thinning, the gaze, signs or the speech.

Layer change in every take: the throne and the stair stand in front of the near band of souls, with their foot in the cloud bank. In the game today the tower is painted before the last far band, which hides it. The tithe line is already painted at this depth.

      Recommendation: take 2, the grand straight stair

      • Take 2, the grand stair, is the only take that reads at both sizes. At 2000 by 967 at rest there are about ten God-cells between his seat and the cloud floor. The stair needs no more than that: the walls, the steps and the flare all show, and the line visibly climbs to his feet. It is also the most one-piece: the stair is the pedestal, so the throne and the stair are one stone object. The approach is head on, which suits the joke: the tithe is worship.
      • Take 1, the cloud switchback, fixes "it reads as dots" outright. The path is cut into a solid cloud cliff, with white treads, grey shadows and turns you can see. It is the best of the three at 800 by 600, where it gets three ledges. At 2000 it shrinks to two short ledges, and the top one sits in the glare. On the dark page the cliff turns grey and reads as rock as much as cloud.
      • Take 3, the spiral, is the most interesting idea, and carriers vanishing behind the stone is the best motion of the three. But it needs height it does not have. At 800 by 600 it reads as barber-pole stripes. At 2000 at rest it gets one turn, mostly under the cloud lip. It would come into its own in the dive, which this mock cannot show.
      • Cost of take 2: the carriers are seen from behind as small backs, so the bowed side view only shows on the walk along the cloud floor. The glare at his feet also covers the hem of his robe. Both are tuning, not a different shape.
