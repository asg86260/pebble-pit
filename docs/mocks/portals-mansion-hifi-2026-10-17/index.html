Portals to heaven and hell, the beats, and the mansion ending

High-fidelity mock, round 2 (2026-10-17), for the owner to iterate on. These are plain cells at the game's sizes (P = 6, a body 18 px with its 2 px rim, the pointing hand at its real ten cells), not the game: nothing here runs the game's renderer. Each scene is drawn at game scale, with a 3x crop beside it that follows its subject. Everything sits on whole cells; the only things finer than a cell are the body's own 2 px rim and the outfits, drawn at the wardrobe's 2 px unit, as in the game. The design is DESIGN.md, "Acts 3 and 4: the afterlife and hell (design, not built)". The portals and the mansion are new proposals (proposal.md in this folder). Every player-facing word in these pictures is a placeholder. Red never appears: hell is hot in black and white, and red stays the sparks'.

The rule the portals keep: a portal is the camera's switch. Clicking it changes the view with the ripple, as the wizards' portal does, and that is the only place the ripple appears. No body ever goes through one. Carriers, ghosts and the freed souls walk the road: down the cloud stair from heaven's tear, along the yard, down the pit and the deep, and down through the slit in the deep's floor. The strips show them walking it.

1. The portal to heaven

The road down from the afterlife (beat 9) is a stair, and the portal is the glide at its top. Once the throne is empty a tear opens over the house's ruin and a stair of cloud lets down from it to the yard floor beside the rock. The ghosts walk the stair; the player's view takes the portal. It stays open after act 3, the way between the yard and the union's camp. It is the wizards' portal's opposite: a white mouth in the white sky, a soft cloud rim, a slow calm fringe turning round it (each cell easing on and off, never blinking), and purple motes drawn up into it. Those four things are heaven's alone; hell never has them. The mouth is a light source: on the dark page it is drawn landed, as hell's heat is, so it stays light on both pages (before and after just below). The pointing hand is the yard portal's hand, and it fades by opacity once the first round trip is made, as S.portalHand does now.

H-A. The tear and the stair (recommended)

The tear over the ruin; the stair comes down from its lower lip in steps two cells tall with an ink rim under each tread, and lands on the yard floor beside the rock. Union ghosts walk down it, pale, and off along the yard toward the pit. The hand stands in the sky to the tear's right, pointing left at it. At heaven's end the stair goes down through a hole in the cloud floor with the yard seen far below; the hand points down into it.

H-B. The column of light

An upright lens standing on the ground beside the ruin, a beam of pale cells going up out of its top. Two problems, kept here for the vote: it has no road of its own (carriers would still need H-A's stair, so H-B is a camera door only), and a white upright shape with light rising off it reads as a white flame, which is hell's signifier in act 4.

2. The portal to hell

Burned open by Satan going ahead of them (beat 8): he sinks through, and where he goes down the way burns open. Satan is not designed and is a marked placeholder shape. Hell's look, which heaven never has: hot is white, black veins flow through the mouth, the lip is rock lit from below, embers rise and thin by opacity. A few purple cells on the seam, because Satan's magic tore it. It has no cloud, no stair, no motes and no fringe.

L-A. The deep's floor tears (recommended)

The spec's way (beat 11): the pit, the abyss, the deep, and through the deep's floor. Satan's route there (beat 8): he sinks through the cloud floor, falls through the yard's sky into the drowned pit, sinks down through the abyss and the deep's water, a body the camera can follow the whole way, and burns through the deep's floor where he lands. A wide slit of white heat in the deep's floor that throws its light up onto the deep above it. The deep is drawn in its own tones, a dark field on both pages, as render/deep.js draws it; the deep's end of the wizards' portal is small at the top left for scale. The hand only appears from the descent beat (beat 11), fading in. Then the carriers walk across the floor and climb down a cut ladder through the slit, out of sight into the heat, and come back up the same way.

L-B. The crater by the ruin (camera only)

Satan comes down through the yard's sky and goes into the ground beside the rock, clipped at the ground line as he sinks, and the ground burns open. Camera only: carriers never use it; they still walk the whole road down through the deep, so the carry's length stands as the spec costs it. The bowl is drawn in hell's own tones, so it stays white-hot on the dark page too.

3. The key beats

Each a cutscene in BEATS. Bodies move in held steps; the camera glides smoothly; everything that comes or goes ramps its opacity continuously, cell by cell.

a. The house falls (fall)

The pair on the bench by the door, the window lit. A shadow eases onto the ground cell by cell from the middle out, the rock comes out of the sky (act 1's first picture again) and lands on the house. Boards fly out and land and stay; dust rolls out and thins away.

b. The two souls rise (fall, its second half)

Two souls ease in out of the wreck, dressed as chosen, the outfit in the soul's grey, their rims pale. They lift in held steps; the camera follows them up into the cloud. This is the spec's beat and is before any tear exists; from the tear on, nobody rises or sinks: they walk.

c. The ghost road back (ghost-road, haunt)

Staged on the yard floor: the tear stands over open ground to the right of the ruin, and the stair lands on the floor. The pair walk down it step by step, then along the yard among the living crew, who are at work and do not look up (black rims, generation ticks over them).

d. Lives returned (alive)

In the cage room, the cage cut open. God is a placeholder (nobody has drawn God), its border easing on and off. Purple motes run from God to the pair; each closes a rim piece, the piece fading from the soul's grey to black, and the outfit darkens with them a twelfth at a time. Then they hop. The union souls who mined the way watch, still pale.

e. The walk home (home-again)

Up out of the pit, alive, and the length of the yard to its left end. The mansion (the one picked above) has been going up as a standing job since the cage barrier's stretch began (4c); the walk shows its last courses, and it tops out as they reach the gate, or, if the real sum runs over, while they stand at the gate and watch. The crew fall in behind them as they pass, two files; one volley of fireworks at the gate; then the crew drift back to work, because everything keeps running.

4. The mansion ending

After freeing God, the pair get a mansion many times the old house (the house is 18 cells wide and 15 tall). Three distinct silhouettes, each with something of theirs in view at game scale. Stone in pairs of cells, ink edges, windows that light and go dark by opacity.

4a. Three designs

A. The manor of the rock. Spiky and symmetrical: two six-floor towers with spires and flags, a four-floor middle block with a pediment and a round window, two wings, a fence and an iron gate, hedges, topiary. Built from the rock that killed them: the rock is its pile, broken up a piece a trip; the door's lintel is carved from it and what is left of it, a few cells, stays in the garden. Theirs: the lintel, the chunk, and the old bench under the door with its back, eight cells wide.

B. The house, grown. One huge gable: the old house at three times its size, the same thick posts, the same roof steps, the same chimney smoking, floors of windows in its walls, the old window grown into a bay, and the old door grown into the entrance with a door their size inside it. Its pile is the rock, like every design. Theirs: the whole shape, the bench by the door, the rock's chunk in the garden.

C. The hall of every line. A massive block with a window for every line the yard has had: twenty a floor, so its floors are the lines over twenty and it grows with the yard (the lines control above; 160 lines is eight floors). A balustrade and an attic over the middle; and in front of it, as its gatehouse, the old house rebuilt on its old ground, whose door is the way in (the game's old door is four cells wide, so a body stands in its left three). Its pile is the rock.

4b. Where it stands

All three on the house's own ground at the yard's left end (houseX()), where the walk home already ends. The yard's work runs on to the right of it, untouched. The tear stays open over it, with its stair down to the ground beside it. See the wide shot (4f).

4c. How it is built

A standing job from the start of the cage barrier's stretch, so it runs through real play: when the anchor chains fall (wall-4), a word comes up the road from the cage (a purple mote along the ground to the yard's builders and a placeholder card over them: the on-screen cause), and the builders start. Beat 14 (the cage falls) and beat 15 are cutscenes inside the stretch; the build runs on through them and tops out on the walk home. The builders are the player's builders (4, 8 or 16 above). The pile is the rock that killed them, for every design. The scaffold goes up a level at a time: each pole's six-row section carried up and set, each plank bay carried and laid, each fading in as it is set. Then every piece is carried from the rock, walked to the nearest pole, climbed, walked along the plank and set, fading in over 0.3 s. Bottom course first, so it rises floor by floor. At the top-out the scaffold comes back down the same way, a section and a bay a trip, each fading out as it is lifted, and the garden goes in. The real sum is printed under the strip with its assumptions (a body's walk, the stop to set, the stretch's length); if it runs over the stretch, the pair stand at the gate and watch the last of it top out (3e).

4d. The arrival and the last held shot

Quieter. The gate opens; the freed souls walk down the stair from the still-open tear and stand on the ground (nobody hovers); one volley of fireworks; the crew drift back to work. The pair walk up to the bench under the door and sit, and a window lights. Then the sheet.

4e. The end sheet

The last held shot, under a veil, and the sheet over it. Every word on it is a placeholder; what it says is the owner's.

4f. The final scene, one wide shot

The whole game's history in one frame at game scale: the mansion at the left end with the pair on the bench by its door, the rock's last chunk in the garden, freed souls standing in the garden, the tear still open over it with its stair down to the ground, the crew at work in the middle, the pit at the right with the wizards' portal in its wall. Hell's way is in the deep and not seen from here.

What this mock cannot show

  • The game's renderer: the abyss field's own tones, the wardrobe's real outfits, device-pixel snapping, the real yard's stations and heaps. The stations here are plain boxes and the crew a handful.
  • The real ripple: the game draws the far half on a sheet through a circle (render/ripple.js). Cost: the ripple should snapshot the far place to an offscreen canvas once when the glide starts and draw that through the circle, not redraw the far place every frame as this mock does; and the mansion should be cached on its own canvas and redrawn only when a piece is set, not repainted cell by cell every frame.
  • The dark page here applies the game's lightness map (ink.js) to the mock's own tones, and draws the deep and hell as landed (a dark field on both pages); the game's own abyss tones and its turned ramps are not reproduced.
  • Dark-page check: the tear's mouth is drawn landed, a light source, so it stays light on both pages (the before and after in section 1); in the yard it is still the wizards' portal's opposite, and against the deep's end (a black mouth, landed) it now reads apart. Hell's slit is also white-hot on a dark field; the two keep apart by their own marks: cloud rim, stair, motes and calm fringe against the under-lit lip, veins and embers.
  • The camera: the game's skyCam window, the far yard, and what the screen shows at a real window size. Each strip is a fixed frame.
  • God, Satan, the cage and the union camp: none are designed. God and Satan are plain placeholder shapes.
  • The real sum's inputs are assumptions marked on the page: a body's walk (20 cells/s), a second to set a piece, pieces of twelve cells, and the cage barrier's stretch, which the spec has no number for yet (BARRIER_BLOWS is unwritten).
  • Pace: every strip is compressed. The walk home, the build, the carry and the rise run in seconds here.
  • The carry's real route: the ghosts' walk from the stair's foot along the yard, down the pit and through the deep is only shown in pieces (the stair's foot, the slit's ladder).
  • Sound, the space bar's hurry, and a reload mid-beat.
