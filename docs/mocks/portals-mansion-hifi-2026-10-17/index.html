Portals to heaven and hell, the beats, and the mansion ending

High-fidelity mock, 2026-10-17, for the owner to iterate on. These are plain cells at the game's sizes (P = 6, a body 18 px with a 2 px rim, the pointing hand at its real ten cells), not the game: nothing here runs the game's renderer. Each scene is drawn at game scale, with a 3x crop beside it. The design is DESIGN.md, "Acts 3 and 4: the afterlife and hell (design, not built)". The heaven and hell portals and the mansion are new proposals, not in the spec yet (proposal.md in this folder). Every player-facing word in these pictures is a placeholder. Red never appears: hell is hot in black and white, and red stays the sparks' color.

1. The portal to heaven

It is the ghost road made into a way (beat 9, ghost-road): once the throne is empty, the afterlife's end opens over the house's ruin, the spot the souls rose from, so the road down lands where the house was. It stays open after act 3, the way between the yard and the union's camp, the way the wizards' portal is the way between the yard and the deep. The deep's portal is a black mouth in black liquid with a veil lip; heaven's is its negative: a white mouth in the white sky, rimmed in cloud, with a dashed ink fringe turning round it so it reads on a white sky. Purple only for what the magic gives off: motes drawn up into the mouth. The pointing hand is the yard portal's hand (purple line, white fill, still 0.6 s then pressed a cell for 0.2 s), and it fades by opacity once the first round trip is made, as S.portalHand does now.

H-A. The tear and the stair

A tall lumpy tear in the sky over the ruin, and a stair of cloud from its lower lip down onto the rock lying on the house. The hand stands in the sky to its right, pointing left at it, as the yard portal's hand does. At heaven's end, the stair's top is a hole in the cloud floor with the yard seen far below through it; the hand points down into it. The ripple out of it rings purple, then pale; cloud puffs are thrown off the leading ring.

H-B. The column of light

A narrow upright lens standing on the ground beside the ruin, with a beam of pale cells going up out of its top into the sky, light rising in it. It stands on the ground like a door, so the souls walk in rather than climb, and the beam is seen from anywhere in the yard. At heaven's end the same lens stands on the cloud floor with the yard's ground seen through it. The hand points left at it at both ends.

2. The portal to hell

Opened by Satan going ahead of them (beat 8, hell-told): he sinks through the cloud, and where he lands the way down burns open. Satan is not designed: he is drawn here as a plain placeholder shape, marked. Hot is white: the mouth is white-hot with black veins flowing through it, the lip is rock lit from below, embers go up in held steps and fade by opacity as they rise. A few purple cells on the seam, because Satan's magic tore it. The hand is the same hand, and fades the same way.

L-A. The deep's floor tears (the spec's way)

Beat 11 says the way to hell is the way they already know: the pit, the abyss, the deep, and through the deep's floor. Satan sinks through the deep's floor and leaves a slit of white heat in it. The deep is drawn in its own turned-over tones (black, grey water); the deep's end of the wizards' portal is drawn small at the top left for scale. The hand hangs above the slit pointing down. At hell's end the slit is a hole in the first chamber's ceiling with the deep's water seen through it; the hand points up. The ripple rings are jagged and step, and throw embers.

L-B. The crater by the ruin

Satan comes down through the yard's sky and lands beside the rock on the ruin, and burns a crater into the yard's ground: a scorched ring, cracks running off it, a white-hot bowl with heat rings running inward, smoke and embers going up into the white sky (embers in ink here, since white is lost on white). Heaven's way and hell's way then stand side by side on the old house's ground. It is quicker to reach than L-A but skips beat 11's "the way they already know", so it could instead be a shortcut that opens after the first descent: a call for the owner.

3. The key beats

Each a cutscene in BEATS. Bodies move in held steps; the camera glides smoothly; dust, smoke and souls fade by opacity; nothing pops.

a. The house falls (fall)

The pair on the bench by the door, the window lit. A shadow spreads on the ground, the rock comes out of the sky (act 1's first picture again) and lands on the house. Boards fly out and land and stay; dust rolls out and thins away.

b. The two souls rise (fall, its second half)

Two souls fade in out of the wreck by opacity, dressed as chosen, the outfit in the soul's grey, their rims pale. They lift in held steps; the camera follows them up through the yard's sky and into the cloud.

c. The ghost road back (ghost-road, haunt)

Down the stair from the tear (H-A drawn here), onto the rock on the ruin, and along the yard among the living crew, who are at work and do not look up: black rims, generation ticks over them (three and four strokes: the grandchildren of the crew the pair knew). The pair are pale and a little see-through.

d. Lives returned (alive)

In the cage room, the cage cut open (two bars on the floor). God is a placeholder: nobody has drawn God. Purple motes run from God to the pair, and each one closes a rim piece: twelve pieces, round the square, grey to black, the union's rim build run to its end. Then they hop. The union souls who mined the way down watch, still pale.

e. The walk home (home-again)

Up out of the pit, alive, and the whole length of the yard to its left end, where the builders are raising the mansion (the one picked above) floor by floor. As the pair pass, each of the crew leaves its work and falls in behind in two files, hopping, as the finale did. Fireworks quicken as they near it; it tops out as they reach the gate. The camera follows the pair.

4. The mansion ending

After freeing God, the pair do not get the old house back: they get a mansion, much bigger than the house (the house is 18 cells wide and 15 tall; these are 70 to 120 wide and 40 to 60 tall). Built of the same stuff as everything else: stone in pairs of cells, each course a tone off its neighbors, ink edges, windows that light and go dark by opacity as people move about inside, flags in three held poses.

4a. Three designs

A. The manor on the old ground. Where the house stood, replacing it: a four-floor middle block with a pediment and a round window, two three-floor wings, two six-floor towers with spires and flags, a fence and an iron gate, hedges, topiary, two trees. The rock that killed them is quarried into its stone (the builders break it up; it is the pile).

B. The castle on the hill. A hill raised at the yard's far left, a keep with battlements on it, a tall spire tower and a short one, a turret; a gatehouse at the hill's foot and a stair winding up to the keep's door. The house's ruin is left where it was, below, with the rock on it and the old bench beside it: a memorial they can see from the windows.

C. The long house of every line. The yard's house was a room a body; this is a window a line. A long two-floor gallery each side of a three-floor hall with a bell tower, dormers in the roof, one-floor wings at the ends that would grow outward with the number of lines. Over each upper window, that line's ticks. The old bench by the door.

4b. Where it stands

A and C stand on the house's own ground at the yard's left end (houseX()), where the walk home already ends. B raises the far left into a hill and leaves the ruin standing below it. In every case the yard's work runs on to the right of it, untouched. See the wide shot (4f).

4c. How it is built

The finale's rule, scaled up: the builders carry a piece a trip from the pile (for A, the broken rock), walk it along the ground and up the scaffold, and set it where it goes, standing at it. Bottom course first, right across the whole front, so it goes up floor by floor. The scaffold stood before the walk (put up while the deep half played, as the finale's poles are) and comes down by fading once the last piece is set. Compressed here: sixteen builders, pieces six cells long.

4d. The arrival

The gate opens in held steps, the pair stand at the door; the crew in two files behind them, generation ticks over their heads; the freed souls (the afterlife's dead, pale) come down and gather on the right and in the sky over it. Fireworks off both sides.

4e. The end sheet

The arrival held, the yard under a white veil, and the sheet over it. Every word on it is a placeholder; what it says is the owner's. Its fields are guesses at what a last sheet could count.

4f. The final scene, one wide shot

The whole yard at game scale: the mansion at the left end with the pair on the bench by its door, freed souls in the garden, the crew at work in the middle, the pit at the right with the wizards' portal in its wall, and heaven's tear still open over the mansion, small, as the way up to the union's camp. Hell's way is in the deep and not seen from here.

  • The camera: the game's skyCam window, the far yard, and what the screen shows at a real window size. Each strip is a fixed 720 px frame.
  • God, Satan, the cage and the union camp: none are designed. God and Satan are plain placeholder shapes.
  • Pace: every strip is compressed. The walk home, the build and the rise run in seconds here; in the game they are the finale's pace.
  • Sound, the space bar's hurry, and a reload mid-beat.
  • How the mansion would grow with the yard (C's wings a line at a time), or what the pair do in it afterwards.
