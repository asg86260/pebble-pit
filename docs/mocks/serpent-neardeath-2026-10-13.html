Serpent near-death

Mocks for DESIGN.md, "Endless serpent", after the vote on the power-up: the owner chose B, draws the deep in, and asked for "a little animation of it almost dying, then powering up. anime style." Each emptied bar: the serpent rears (as built), then a near-death beat, then B's power-up (the coil winds tighter, motes cross the water into it, a notch of the bar a mote, the let-go smear), and ends at rest with the bar full and the level ticked. The three differ only in the near-death beat; vote one. Nothing here is built.

This is a mock, drawn from plain shapes on a canvas, not the game. The coil is laid from the game's own numbers (length, head, neck, girth and tail taper, the sway, the crest, the jaws' hinge and gape, the cell grid), the rear is the built one (a smear frame of 0.08 s past the pose, 1.35 times as far, the pose held 0.7 s), and the power-up is B as voted: three held steps tighter, motes crossing in four held steps of 0.1 s with a dim smear behind and a spark on landing, a notch of the bar each, the level ticking on the first, the let-go one smear frame past the rest line. What sits between the rear and the power-up is each option's own, timed below; all three read in 3.3 to 4 s from the empty bar to rest (built today: 1.74 s).
  • As built: the risen serpent's bar has no pips, only the level after it, and its belly no cage of ribs; the mock draws it that way.
  • What it cannot show: the fighters, their blows and numbers, the creatures and the meals, the orb, the statuses and the stun ring, the hide's ward and plates as the resist draws them, the fang dropping from the belly, the real bar's shards and its pixel-font level (the number here is the page's font), the stations' water and the deep's floor (the slack body sinks into open water here; in the game it would come down onto whatever lies under it), the camera, and how long the fighters stand idle through a 3 to 4 s beat; the body is laid along x rather than along its arc, so a steep bend is a little thinner here than in the game.
  • The rest line (an aid for this page, not in the game): where the body would be at rest, in dark grey behind, through the whole beat. Pause to hold a frame; 1/4x to read the steps.
  • No shake, no white flash, no squash tween: every frame is a held pose on the cell grid; a smear is one held frame past the pose; the sway is held still from the last blow to the rest. Greys and the deep's purples only (the ward's ramp).

A. Goes grey, then the eye

Out of the rear the body goes slack: it sags toward the floor in three held steps, the sway flattening and the head drooping lowest, while the hide greys out a sixth at a time from the tail to the head, the crest last. At the bottom the eye shuts to a dim slit. A held beat of stillness. Then the eye snaps open purple with a glint struck across it for one held frame, and the motes start; the body rises back in three steps as the coil winds tighter, and the grey lifts from head to tail as the bar fills.

Timing
rear 0.78 (smear 0.08, held 0.7); the sag 0.45 (three steps of 0.15, the grey in six of 0.075); still 0.6; the eye 0.12; the power-up 1.4 (30 motes); the let-go 0.08. 3.43 s.
Reads as
The classic "the light goes out of its eyes", and the eye is what comes back first; the most legible of the three at a glance.
Cost
A grey serpent for a second can read as a status (a stun or a slow) rather than near-death; the sag brings the belly down onto the fighters' heads.

B. Last breath

Out of the rear the head falls to the floor in three held steps, the last one landing three cells past and held there as the impact, jaws ajar. A long held frame: nothing moves. Then one heartbeat: a single purple pulse runs out from the belly to the snout and to the tail in held steps, a dim band behind its front. When it reaches the ends the power-up erupts: the head is thrown back up in one smear frame, crest lit, and three purple outline rings step out from the body one after another, each fading as it goes; the motes come and the head comes down in three steps as in B.

Timing
rear 0.78; the fall 0.36 (three steps of 0.12, the impact 0.06 of the last); silence 0.7; the pulse 0.48 (six steps of 0.08); the eruption 0.24 (smear 0.08, held 0.16), the rings over 0.64 from it (three rings 0.12 apart, five steps of 0.08); the power-up 1.3 (30 motes); the let-go 0.08. 3.94 s, the longest.
Reads as
Dead, and then not: the silence is the beat the player is meant to doubt, and the pulse says what is about to happen before it does.
Cost
The longest, and 0.7 s of nothing is long in an idle game the second time; the rings cross the fighters as the motes do.

C. Breaks, then the light comes through

Out of the rear the head comes down in three steps and the wound's cracks run from the belly over the whole body in five held steps, the body slackening, until the hide is crazed snout to tail. Held on the brink, while dark focus lines close in from the frame's edge in three steps. Then the cracks fill with purple light from the inside, out from the belly in four held steps, as the motes rush in; each crack seals from its tips back toward the belly as the bar fills, a bright seam at the sealing edge, and the coil lets go whole.

Timing
rear 0.78; the cracks 0.5 (five steps of 0.1, the head down in three of 0.12); the brink 0.55 (the focus lines in three steps of 0.18); the power-up 1.4 (30 motes; the light out in four steps of 0.08, the lines gone in two of 0.16); the let-go 0.08. 3.31 s, the shortest.
Reads as
Breaking apart and mended by the deep; it grows out of the wound the player has been opening all level, so the near-death is the fight's own damage.
Cost
The busiest frame of the three on the brink (cracks and lines at once); the lines darken the stations' water at the edges of the deep.
