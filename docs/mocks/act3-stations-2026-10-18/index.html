Act 3 stations: the yard's simple feel

A mock, drawn from plain cells on the game's grid (P = 6, the game's greys, a body an 18-pixel square with a 2-pixel rim), not the game. Each station is drawn at 1x game scale, idle and working, with a 3x crop of each. Stations are the size of the yard's (about 8 to 12 cells across), flat black shapes, grey only for material (sawdust, pulp, a log's cut face, soil). Over each working station the yard's floating glyph stack hangs, with one upgrade half built (buildingGlyph: built cells black from the bottom up, the rest a light grey outline). The saw mill has a third frame, full, with the yard's pile-full mark under the heap where pileMarkAt puts a strip's mark. Inputs and outputs are counted piles beside each station; the number is the yard's count font with its paper key.

Every word on this page is a placeholder. Station names, option names and pile names are working names (marked like this). The canvases draw no words, only numbers. The owner writes the player's words.

Motion is held poses in the classic style: a hammer up, swinging with a grey smear, held on the strike; a wheel in stepped turns. Anything arriving or leaving (a log taken off a pile, a sheet landing on a stack, a crown after the fall) fades by opacity. Nothing pops.

Dark is ink #e0e0e0 on paper #1f1f1f, each grey mapped by its lightness the way ink.js maps it (black to the ink, white to the paper, a grey to the same step between). It is not an inversion.

Recommendations

    What the mock cannot show

    • The real camp: the cloud floor, the crowd, God's yard, the pedestal and the stair. Each station stands alone on a plain ground line.
    • The real glyphs. The 8 by 8 drawings over the stations are placeholders for upgrades nobody has drawn; the coin-colored stroke round a built glyph is left off, since act 3's coins have no colors yet. The stack's drift is left off too.
    • The count font in screen pixels. The game draws counts at two screen pixels a font pixel at any zoom; here the 3x crops blow the 1x picture up, so the numbers are three times too big there.
    • Haulers and carriers walking logs, timber and paper between stations. Inputs fade off their piles and outputs fade onto theirs; in the game a body carries every one.
    • The player's motions themselves (spinning the flywheel, stirring, pulling the bar, the hammer taps, feeding the chipper, the two-person saw). They are being mocked separately. The dotted shapes only show the room each look leaves for them.
    • The build: a station raised in bands a piece at a time, each fading in. Each station here is finished.
    • Posts, the roster under the ground line, the boards, and a station with more than one worker.
    • The real pile capacities and rates. A pile here holds a few things so its fill can be seen in seconds.
    • Per-cell variation on anything but the heaps, the pulp and the soil. A real station is flat black like the yard's.
