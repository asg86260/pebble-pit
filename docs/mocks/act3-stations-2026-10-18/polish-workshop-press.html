Act 3 polish: the workshop and the press

A mock drawn from plain cells on the game's grid (P = 6, the game's greys, a body an 18-pixel square with a 2-pixel rim), not the game. It follows the picks: workshop A (lean-to) and press A (screw press), and the owner's notes: they don't read well at our low cell size; exaggerate each station and focus on the thing it does.

The rule used on both: the part that does the work is drawn far past life size and everything else is cut to what holds it up. The workshop is now a hammer with a roof over it; the press is now a screw and a platen with a frame round them. One bold black outline each, no single loose cells, one white cut-out (the sign's face, the sheet), grey only where the motion smears.

Every word on this page is a placeholder (marked like this). Motion is held poses: the hammer held up, one smeared swing frame, held on the strike; the bar and platen in three held steps. Anything arriving or leaving (a sign, a sheet, the strike's lines) fades by opacity. Nothing pops.

Dark is ink #e0e0e0 on paper #1f1f1f, each grey mapped by its lightness (black to the ink, white to the paper, a grey to the same step between). It is not an inversion.

Calls made here

  • The hammer's head is a 5 by 3 block (30 by 18 pixels; the old head was 18 by 6), wider than the body. At 1x it is the first black mass you see.
  • Every hammer pose is drawn by hand in whole cells, not rotated. A 5 by 3 head turned to 45 degrees was tried first and came apart into a ragged blob at this size. Three poses: up (straight up, the head a T over the body, held), a smear frame (the head only grey, faint where it was and darker half way down), and the strike (the head stood on its face on the sign, the handle a clean 4-pixel line back to the hand, held, two short strokes off the face fading over the hold). Idle, the hammer lies on the sign where the last blow left it.
  • The body stands on a low step at the bench. A blow comes down, so the hand has to be above what it hits; a body is three cells tall, so the bench is low and the body is raised one cell. The sign stands on the bench as a white board, the station's one white cut-out. No lettering on it: lettering at this size is grit.
  • The lean-to is cut to thin lines (a 3-pixel roof and two 3-pixel posts), high over the raised hammer so the head never touches it. This is the one place the page leaves the yard's solid masses: a solid roof was the heaviest black on the station and the first thing read, which is what the owner asked to fix. Say if the roof should be solid again.
  • The press's screw is three cells wide (18 pixels; the old one was 6) with white threads cut across it as clean 2-pixel slants, and it stands up out of the head under a cap. Pulled, the screw, the bar and the platen drop two cells together (12 pixels; the old platen moved 3), so the screw visibly sinks into the head: the cause and the effect are one motion.
  • The bar is straight and rides the screw between the head and the platen, out past the right post (keyed in white where it crosses, so post and bar stay two shapes), then down a rod to a knob at the body's hand. A bar slanting down to the hand was tried first; its stair ran into the platen. Pulled, the body crouches on its step with the knob: the player's drag down is the knob's drop.
  • The glyph stack is left off both before and after here, so only the stations' own reads are compared. The old press's squeeze lines are gone too: the drop says it.

What the mock cannot show

  • The real camp (the cloud floor, the crowd, God's yard, the pedestal). Each station stands alone on a plain ground line.
  • The game's own scale. The game draws a cell at 5 screen pixels at its usual zoom; this page's 1x is 6, so it is 1.2 times the game. The yard shot is scaled up to match, nearest-neighbour, which leaves the odd doubled pixel in its 2-pixel rims.
  • The player's motions themselves (the hammer tapped on the beat, the bar dragged down). The dotted shapes only show the room each look leaves for them.
  • Carriers walking timber, signs, paper and posters between stations. Inputs fade off their piles and outputs fade onto theirs; in the game a body carries every one.
  • The build in bands, posts, the roster, the boards, the glyph stack, and a station with more than one worker.
  • The real pile capacities and rates.
