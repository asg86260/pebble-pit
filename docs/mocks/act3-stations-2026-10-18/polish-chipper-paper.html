Act 3 stations, polished: chipper and paper mill

A mock, drawn from plain cells on the game's grid (P = 6, the game's greys, a body an 18-pixel square with a 2-pixel rim), not the game. It polishes the two picks from the act 3 stations mock (index.html): chipper B, the gooseneck drum, and paper mill B, the vat and chimney. The owner's notes were that they did not read well at low resolution, and to exaggerate each station and keep only the thing it does.

What changed: the signature part is drawn well past realistic and everything else is cut back to what holds it up. Each station is one bold black mass with one white cut-out, and grey only for material (chips, pulp, a log's end). No single loose cells; every detail is at least two cells or a clean line. Each shows before (the old pick, unchanged) and after, at 1x and a 2x crop, idle and working, plus the working poses side by side.

Every word on this page is a placeholder (marked like this). The canvases draw no words, only numbers. Motion is held poses only; anything arriving or leaving fades.

Dark is ink #e0e0e0 on paper #1f1f1f, each grey mapped by its lightness (black to the ink, white to the paper, a grey to the same step between). It is not an inversion. The yard station in the comparison row is mapped the same way.

Calls made

  • The chipper's mouth is an open jaw, not a funnel. Drawn side on, a funnel is a closed black triangle and its opening cannot be seen; an open wedge (a top jaw sloping down to the drum, the bottom jaw flat on the ground) is white inside and open to the left, so the log is seen going into it. The lip stands over four bodies high. No teeth, so it does not turn into a face (the reason jaws, option C, lost).
  • The chipper loses its wheels. The drum sits on the ground as one block; the wheels were two loose cells at 1x. The pipe is two cells thick, rises well over the drum and hooks round in a half circle, and the chips pour out of its spout as a dashed grey line straight onto the heap, so cause and effect are one picture.
  • The paper mill drops its chimney. The long paddle now gives the station its tall line, and the chimney was a second thing to read. A frame with the chimney kept is shown under the after row, so it can be voted back in.
  • The vat is a cutaway. Thick black walls and floor, rounded at the bottom, the pulp seen through as grey, with one white band of air under the rim: the one white cut-out. The body stands on a black post level with the rim, so the post and the vat are one mass, and the paddle rests on the rim's corner like an oar on a gunwale.
  • The stir is a loop in four held poses. The blade goes in at the near side, along under the surface, out at the far side and back deep: an ellipse seen side on. The pulp moves with it: a grey crest stands up into the air ahead of the blade and a white trough opens behind it. The dotted ellipse is the room for the player's circular stir.
  • The feed is a push in held steps. The body walks the log into the jaw a cell at a time, leans on the last step, the log fades into the throat, and the body walks back a cell at a time. Nothing jumps. The dotted box is the room for the player's hold.

What the mock cannot show

  • The real camp: the cloud floor, the crowd, the other act 3 stations. Each station stands alone on a plain ground line.
  • The player's motions themselves (holding to feed, stirring in circles). The dotted shapes only show the room each drawing leaves for them.
  • Haulers. Logs fade onto the body's hands at the pile and sheets fade onto the stack; in the game a body carries every one.
  • The real glyphs over the stations (placeholders, nobody has drawn them), the build in bands, posts and the roster.
  • The count font in screen pixels: the 2x crops blow the 1x picture up, so their numbers are twice too big.
  • The real pile capacities and rates.
