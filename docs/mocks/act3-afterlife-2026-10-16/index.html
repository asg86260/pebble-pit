Act 3: the afterlife, the look

Plain cells at the game's sizes (P = 6, a body 18 with a 2-pixel rim), not the game. Each scene at game scale and a 3x crop. Design: DESIGN.md, "Act 3: the afterlife (design, not built)". The theme: the afterlife is like real life, and you keep working. Revised from docs/mocks/act3-heaven-2026-10-16.

1. The greeting (every loop)

The pair's souls come down onto the cloud, dressed as chosen (defaults: top hat, monocle, bowtie; bow, eyes, scarf), fading in by opacity in held steps. The crowd of the dead stands in files by line, rims in the pale grey, ticks over the head; the near files hop. Then one of the front file walks out carrying a pick to the pair: no words needed, the pick is the line.

A + B. The yard in the clouds, staffed by the roll call (recommended)

A's yard, read at a glance as the yard again: a cloud hit like the rock, mist falling to the floor, a heap, two station boxes. B's crew: no hire rung; every worker is one of the dead, rebuilt a rim piece a click at the gate on the right, then walks to a station. Ticks over a worker are its line's depth, the trade it keeps.

A. The cloud is the rock

High key: a great cloud hit like the rock, mist falling as pale grains onto a cloud floor; woken crew have black rims, the waiting dead pale ones. Under the new theme its likeness to act 1 is the joke; alone, the dead are only a hire pool.

B. The roll call

A soul is rebuilt a rim piece a click (twelve pieces round the square), then walks off a body. The field goes from grey to ink as the act is played. The first draft's pick; alone it reads as a crowd, not as work, so the theme wants it inside A's yard.

C. The climb

The afterlife is tall: shelves of cloud with thin black edges, a stair to each, the gate a purple outline at the top, light falling from it. A climb to a gate is the paradise story, the opposite of the theme.

What this cannot show

  • The game's own renderer: the wardrobe's device-pixel snapping, the scarf's tail, the class kits on the deep's dead, the yard's real crowd ranks.
  • The beat itself (the house destroyed, the rise through the yard's sky) and the camera.
  • A crowd of thousands: the far files thinning into the cloud is described, not drawn.
  • Dark mode here swaps ink and paper and reverses the greys, keeping purple; the game's dark-mode treatment of a new place is undecided.
  • Any loop's numbers, boards or pacing, and the boards' cards in the hybrid (the station boxes are placeholders).
  • How literal the mirror is: the hybrid shows the close mirror; the turned systems (rising mist, the yard's smog below, a glare) are not drawn.
  • The yard below showing through the cloud.
  • Any words a player would read: none are written here; they are the owner's.
