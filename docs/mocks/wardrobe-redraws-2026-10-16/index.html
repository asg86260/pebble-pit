Wardrobe redraws (mock)

Three of the owner's marks from the item review, drawn here from plain shapes on a bare 18-pixel body: the rim 2 px, the white middle 14 px, a cell 6 px, a face or neck mark a 2 px unit. The "today" rows are copied from the real item rows in src/wardrobe.js and src/render/wardrobe.js. Each picture is shown at 4x and at 1x (one world pixel to one screen pixel). Walking panels step their pose four times a second, as the game does.

1 · Faces: directional vs centered

Today a face is drawn facing right and mirrored for a body facing left, so the eyes sit off to the side it walks toward. Centered: the face sits on the middle of the square's front and is the same whichever way the body faces. Asymmetric faces (monocle, eyepatch) keep one fixed side when centered rather than flipping.

2 · Scarves: proportions

Today the band is one 2 px row across the middle but the tail is drawn in whole 6 px cells (two hanging, three streaming), three times the band's weight: that is the mismatch. Each option is applied to all three scarves, standing and walking, facing right.

3 · Bow: redraws

Today the bow is three cells tall and five wide (30 x 18 px) over an 18 px body. The redraws are laid in 2 px units, as the headband already is, rather than whole cells.

What this mock cannot show

  • The yard: no crowd, no station hats, no camera zoom. "1x" is one world pixel per screen pixel; the yard is usually drawn larger than that, so 1x is the worst case.
  • The deep's inks (the picture turned over, its own greys), a body knocked flat (where the head item is laid on its side), a crouched or seated body, or a load carried over the head.
  • The real walk: the body here stands in place while its pose steps; in the game it moves across the yard and the "walking" flag is read off its motion.
  • Device-pixel snapping at odd zooms, which the game does for every mark.
