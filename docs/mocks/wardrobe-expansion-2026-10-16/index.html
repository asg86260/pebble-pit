The wardrobe, more: pieces earned by achievements, and a grid to pick from

Mock, not the game. The same drawing code as the aura mock (docs/mocks/wardrobe-aura-2026-10-16): a body is the 3 x 3 cell square (18 world pixels) with a 2-pixel rim, a cell is 6 world pixels, small parts and particles are laid in 2-pixel units (WARD_UNIT) on a world-anchored grid. "4x" is four screen pixels to a world pixel; "game scale" is one to one. Every particle fades by opacity; the canvases run on held frames, 12 a second. Names marked like this are placeholders for the owner: every new item name and every lock line.

Part 1: new pieces, each opened by an achievement

Nineteen pieces across all six slots, each with the achievement that would open it (name and note are the game's, locales/en.ftl). Where the game already draws the thing, the piece is that drawing made small: the dizzy stars are the dizzy mark's two cells going round the head (render/crew.js), the stink is the "yuck" mark's wafting risers, the birds are the sky's birds (a body and two wingtips that swap from V to caret, BIRD_TONE), the shooting star is the glints' plus. Gold is used once, on the piece for sqouse freed, the serpent kill (treatment A: one gold, earned only).

Part 1b: the station hats, wearable

The owner: each of the station hats should be an option to wear, and each opens when that hat is unlocked for its station, not by an achievement. These are the real sprites (HATS in src/sprites.js) on the bare body, placed as the crew's are (centered on the body, drawHat in render/crew.js). The carter's is not a hat (the cart, KIT[HAUL].mark), and the deep's classes wear nothing on the head (the "inspired" chevron is a buff mark over a body, not kit), so these five are all of them.

The game fact each hat waits on

The kit table (KIT in src/kit.js) says where each station's hats come from, and the rule here is the first one of them the yard owns: stockOf(job) >= 1. For the four bought hats that is the first hat bought on the station's kit row; for the janitor's cap, which nobody buys, it is the outhouse opening (its shed hangs one on the stand for every post).

  • Why the first bought and not the row showing: a kit row is on the board before anyone can afford it, and the yard has never seen the hat on a head. The first one bought is the first one a body walks to the stand and puts on, so the hat is opened by the moment the player first sees it worn. The other reading (the row opening) is one line to change.
  • "First hat" overlaps: the achievement firsttrade ("first hat") fires on the first of the four bought hats, any station; this rule is per station. The wizard's point opens at the same moment as "you're a wizard squarey" (firsthat), so no separate wizard hat is drawn for that achievement.
  • A stock is not kept: the outhouse can close and the counts can fall (a forklift displacing carts, an old save). Once a hat is opened for the wardrobe it should stay opened: a flag kept in the save, set the first time the rule holds, not read off the count each time.

What reads, and what I would watch

  • Read at game scale: the flies (the only thing in the yard that moves that fast round one body), the stink risers, the juggled balls, the dizzy stars, the bird on the head, the mortarboard, the diving bowl, the gold-trimmed cape.
  • Read only close up: the pocket watch and the surprised face (both are a few units inside the body), the patches; the speed lines read only while walking, which is the point of them.
  • Flies and stink belong together. Both are for muck; worn as one look they read at once as "this one smells". They are two slots' worth of one idea only if the aura slot holds one thing: worn together they need two auras, or one piece "flies and stink". Drawn here as two, opened by two achievements; the crowd's "muck" look shows them combined.
  • The flock sits where the sky's real birds fly; at game scale two small birds over one head is read, but next to a passing lot of real ones it may be taken for one of them.
  • Brown was not used. The muck has its own tone (MUCK_TONE, a brown, config/weather.js); flies or stink in it would read as muck at once, but that is a second hue on a wardrobe that keeps hue for earned gold. Grey here; the brown is one line if wanted.
  • The potion fizz is grey. The apothecary's brews each have a color; a fizz in a brew's color would say "potion" better and break the same rule.

Part 2: the appearance page as a grid

Every option in a slot at once, each a small tile of the bare body wearing it (2x, standing; walking in place while hovered, focused or chosen, so a cape streams). The chosen one is inverted: black tile, the body drawn in the deep's turned-over inks. A locked one is faint with a lock; hovering or focusing it puts its achievement (or its station's rule) on the line under the grid. Picking a locked tile tries it on, faint, on the close-up, and is not kept, as option B of the aura mock's locked look; randomize never rolls one. Tick an achievement to pretend it is earned.

How a pad moves in the grid

Try it with the pad buttons under each desk sheet, or the keyboard with the pointer over a sheet: arrows the d-pad, Enter A, y Y, q / e LB / RB, [ / ] LT / RT.

  • The d-pad goes to the nearest button in that direction, measured off the page as drawn, not off a list: of everything whose middle lies that way, the one nearest along the direction, with anything lined up with the current one (overlapping across) preferred over anything off to the side. So right along a row of tiles, down to the tile under, down off the last row to the line of buttons, up off the first row to the tabs. It works the same in both layouts and on any width, because it never knows the grid has columns.
  • No wrap: right off the end of a row goes nowhere. Wrapping to the next row is what a list does and a grid should not; the next row is down.
  • A picks the tile under the focus (a locked one is tried on, not kept). Y rolls the slot the focus is in. LB / RB are you and your sqouse, as today. LT / RT are the slot tabs in A, and in B jump the focus to the first tile of the previous or next section, scrolling it into view.
  • The name and the role: unchanged from today. The name field takes A to type (Steam's keyboard, or the letter grid, under it); the role keeps its previous and next. Both sit beside the close-up in the head of the page, so the grid below is all wardrobe.
  • Randomize and clear: the whole-square roll and the clear sit at the foot, as today; the per-slot die moves from each row to the slot's heading (A: beside the tabs, B: on each section's head).

The two layouts, side by side

  • A, tabs: one slot at a time, so the page is short and the close-up and the grid are always on the glass together; the cost is a tab press to see another slot, and the tab bar on a phone (six tabs) has to scroll sideways. On a pad, LT / RT is a natural "next slot".
  • B, sections: everything at once, so a player sees all there is to earn in one scroll, and the page reads as a catalog; the cost is length (about eighty tiles), so the close-up has to be held (left column on the desk, a sticky strip on the phone) or the player loses sight of what they are wearing while choosing. On a pad the d-pad alone walks the whole page.
  • Both drop the counts ("3 / 12"): the grid shows the count.

What this mock cannot show

  • The real yard: rocks, heaps, the sky's motes, grains, rain and real birds, carts and loads over the head. The flock and the flies will be harder to find against a real sky; the stink rises where speech marks and status icons sit.
  • The deep's real palette: here it is the yard's greys flipped by hand, not render/deeptones.js's capped ramp.
  • The real zoom and its snapping of 2-pixel units at fractional zooms.
  • Poses: crouched, carried, knocked flat, the snatch, the finale; the bird would need to fly off a body that is knocked flat, the bowler to fall with a shaken hat.
  • Two auras at once (flies and stink together) is drawn in the crowd only; the slot model holds one.
  • The real sheet: drawn by hand to look like the game's, not with src/style.css; the real pad's focus look (padpage.js) and its scroll-into-view; the letter grid for the name; the new yard's four steps (build, confirm, build, both).
  • Cost in the game: the counts are rects per body per frame from this page; about eighty tile canvases drawn at once in B is a page cost the game would pay only while the sheet is up.
  • How an unlock is told to the player (a notice, a mark on the board's row). Not drawn.
