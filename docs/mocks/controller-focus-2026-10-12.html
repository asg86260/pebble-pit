Focus on a pad

Every part of the game as it looks with a controller in hand. Focus moves between the places a click would land, and a marker in the purple accent says where it is. Three markers to choose from; the picker below redraws every panel in the one you pick. The first panel shows all three side by side.

This is a mock. Drawn from plain shapes, not the game: the stations are blocks on a 6-pixel grid, the boards, sheets and cards are stand-ins built to the game's shapes, and no real pad is read. It cannot show the real art, the real board stylesheet, how the marker reads over rain, smog or a busy endgame yard, or how a pad feels in the hand. Every word in a prompt or tip is a placeholder for the owner's own.

1 · The three markers, hopping along the yard

The d-pad hops one stop along the yard. The marker glides there in a quarter second, fast out and soft in, on whole cells only, then steps in a cell or two to land. The camera follows behind it at a slower glide; it never cuts. The name the hover gives today comes up once the marker has settled.

2 · Every surface, in the picked marker

What the three markers are

A · Corner brackets. Four L-shaped corners a cell outside the thing, three cells along each arm. When it lands it steps in from two cells out, then breathes out by one cell and back on a slow two-pose beat. It says what is selected without covering any of it, and it scales from a roster's plus sign to the whole rock.

B · Stepped outline. A full one-cell ring with its corners knocked out, the pixel-art rounded box. It is the plainest and the strongest read on a busy yard. It covers the most picture of the three.

C · Bobbing arrow. The game's own chevron (the shaft arrow and the orb's arrow), hanging over the thing and bobbing a whole cell on a slow beat. It covers nothing, and it reads as "this one" the way the existing hints do. On a small target in a crowd (one body among six, a single coil segment) it is the least precise about which one.

All three wear a one-cell halo in the paper color, so the accent reads over ink, over grey and over the deep's dark. In the deep the accent is the lighter purple the deep already uses.
