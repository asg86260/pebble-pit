1 · the two-person saw: three takes

A long saw across the trunk: blade, teeth, a handle at each end, your partner on the far one. Every take is level and plumb; the falling tree turns only in held steps of 45 degrees, so no line is ever at an odd angle. The first card of each take is live (drag the near handle back and forth: a stroke end to end is a chop, eight fell it); the other three are its idle, mid-stroke and falling frames.

2 · the card system: three directions

The same eight cards in each direction: the flywheel, the vat, the press bar, the feed button, the hammer pad, the saw, the stump grip, the sawdust load. The speed gauge only ever fills with speed (faster is more, no band to aim for); progress counts toward the next thing made; the ghost hand fades out after three goes; the crit is a held pose, no flash, no shake.

3 · the stump

The stump never moves by itself: it moves only while you pull its grip up, and settles back when you let go. When a stump can be dug, a shovel fades in over it in the camp, drawn as the yard's glyphs and pile marks are. Its crit, with no wobble to time it to, is one of two:

crit
  • (a) Hold the grip up past the line and the stump strains up a cell, then another, then a root snaps: the broken root is held open for six tenths of a second, and a yank (a quick pull higher) in that moment tears the rest out at once. Every moment is one you made; nothing comes on its own clock.
  • (b) Hold the grip up and a root snaps every seven tenths of a second, three and it is out. The pedestal's big bubble is the only crit left in the act.

Recommend (a): it keeps the second crit the owner voted for, and it is caused by your own pull, so it teaches itself. (b) is the fallback if (a) reads as fiddly on a phone.

What the mock cannot show

  • The real renderer: these are cells drawn by this page, not render/aftercard.js; the camp is a strip of stand-in stations at the camp's scale, not after.js's place, its crowd or its members walking.
  • The card on the glass: here each card stands in its own box; in the game it hangs over its station, eases aside for a neighbor and rises in as it opens. The hand-control mock showed that, and it is unchanged.
  • The house face: Courier New stands in for Courier Prime, so the head and tiles are a few pixels off.
  • Real numbers: the speeds, caps and counts are the built defaults copied in; the purse, the posts and the bills are a made-up mid-act yard, and buying only changes numbers (in the game, coins fly up and a member walks to the post).
  • The grove: the tree and the stump come back here on their own after a few seconds so the card can be worked again; in the game the tree falls into the grove's own cycle.
  • Gamepad feel: none of this reads a pad. The pad lines are the card-tabs mock's (LT / RT switch tabs).
  • A real phone: the page narrows and the cards' planks scroll sideways, but thumb reach is not shown.
  • The "now" crops are light-page captures, mapped by lightness for the dark page; the game's own dark canvas may differ by a step.
