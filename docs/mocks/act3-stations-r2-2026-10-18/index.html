Act 3 stations, second redesign: matched to the press

A mock, drawn from plain cells on the game's grid (P = 6, the game's eleven greys, a body the game's 18-pixel square with a 2-pixel rim), not the game. Every station sits on its white paper backing over a stand-in for the camp's crowd, the way it stands in the camp. Each is drawn at 1x game scale, idle and working, with a 3x crop of the working pose. The press is drawn as built and is the bar; everything else is redrawn three ways. Each station's "now" is redrawn from src/render/after/stations.js and pine.js with the same numbers.

Every word on this page is a placeholder (marked like this): station names, take names and pile names are working names. The canvases draw no words, only counts. The owner writes the player's words.

Dark is ink #e0e0e0 on paper #1f1f1f, each grey mapped by its lightness (black to the ink, white to the paper, a grey to the same step between). It is not an inversion.

Why the press reads

Measured off the built drawing and the 3x shot of the camp, against the workshop beside it. The guess was right on four counts and wrong on one (symmetry matters less than a closed outline):

  1. A closed outline. Two posts, a two-cell head and a two-cell bed make one rectangle. Its outline is a box with feet, so at 1x it is "a machine standing there" before any detail reads. The workshop's outline is two 3-pixel hairlines and a sloped roof line; it is open on every side, so it reads as scribble.
  2. One big moving part on the middle line. The screw is 3 cells wide and 10 tall and moves 2 whole cells with the platen. The moving part is the largest single mass in the picture, so the eye lands on it first.
  3. Solid masses cut with white, never drawn in outline. The screw's threads are white slants cut out of black; the sheet is a white strip cut into the bed; the bar is keyed white where it crosses the post. Every shape is at least a cell thick, and the only thin line (the handle) is short and straight up and down.
  4. Right angles only. Every edge on the press is level or plumb except the thread slants, which are a cut-out pattern inside a mass. The workshop has a sloped roof, a diagonal handle and a hammer that lands at an angle. Three different angles in one small drawing are the "wonky".
  5. The body is wired to the part. The bar runs out of the frame to a handle at the body's hand, and the body squats as the platen drops. Cause and effect are one straight line you can follow at 1x. Symmetry helps (the frame is mirrored) but the body stands outside it, so the whole is not symmetric. A closed outline matters more than a mirror.
  6. The product shows inside the machine. The white sheet sits on the bed under the platen, so what the station makes is visible where it is made.

Every take below is held to those six: a closed or near-closed outline, one moving part that is the largest mass or the thing the eye follows, white cut-outs instead of outlines, level and plumb edges (one moving rod or pole may slant), a body joined to the part by a straight link, and the product in view.

Recommendations

    What the mock cannot show

    • The real camp: the cloud floor's billow edge, God's yard behind, the stair, the posts along each station's top, the build in bands, and a station with more than one worker. The crowd behind is a stand-in pattern copied by eye from a shot.
    • The player's hand. Spinning the flywheel, holding the log in the mouth, stirring, the hammer taps and the two-person saw are each shown only as the worker's loop; how a drag or a hold maps onto these parts (the hand's boxes in after/place.js) is not tested here. Each take keeps a part the hand can take hold of where the motion names one.
    • The glyph stack over a working station, the counts in screen pixels (here they are drawn at 1x with the picture, so the 3x crops blow them up), and the pile-full mark.
    • Carriers walking logs, timber and paper between stations: inputs fade off their piles and outputs fade on.
    • The sawdust heap shared by the saw mill and the chipper. Each take here pours onto its own small heap where its spout is; a take whose spout moves would move the heap's place in ART.
    • The grove's growth stages, the fall in held poses, the crown lying and fading, and the stump being dug out. Only the grown tree, the sawing and a stump are drawn.
    • The chipper turned to face the saw mill is drawn turned here too (it faces left), as in the game.
    • Whether the new sizes fit the camp's spacing. Each take keeps to about the old box (20 to 26 cells across, 14 to 20 high), but the ART boxes, doors and pile spots would all be re-read.
