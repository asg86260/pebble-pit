Settings, in sections

Three ways to break the settings page into graphics, audio and controls (the owner's three words). Every row is a row the game has today, with its own words (locales/en.ftl); "the two of you" is left out, since it is moving to the house board. One extra section was needed for the rows that are none of the three: it is drawn as other -- a dotted underline and * mark a name nobody has written yet. Each option is drawn on the held sheet at the desk's width and as the phone's sheet from the bottom; the purple corners are where a pad's focus stands. This page is drawn from plain shapes, not the game. Click the rows: A's sections open and go back, B's tabs switch.

today's sheet, for comparison: today-*.png beside this page

A · A list of sections, each a page

Settings opens on a short list -- graphics, audio, controls, other -- and each opens its own page with its rows and a back. The controls page is the controls section itself: the touch switch over the grouped table. Back returns to the list; back on the list returns to the front.

Pad: front ↓ settings, A → the list (↓↑ between four rows) → A on a section → its rows (↓↑, ←→ on the volume) → B back to the list, the focus on the section you left → B to the front. Two presses deeper than today for every switch.

B · Tabs across the top

One settings page; a strip of tabs under the word, the rows of the tab in force below it. The controls tab is the touch switch over the grouped table. One back at the foot goes to the front. The tab strip is the boards' marking: the chosen one inverted.

Pad: front ↓ settings, A → graphics, the focus on its first row → LB / RB turn the tab (the same buttons turn a board in the yard), the focus to the new tab's first row → ↓↑ rows, ←→ on the volume → B to the front. ↑ off the first row lands on the tab strip, where ←→ also turn it, so a keyboard gets there too.

C · One page, with headings

Today's page with every row put under a heading, in the controls table's own heading style; the controls table sits inline under its heading rather than behind a button. Taller than the window on a phone and on a short desk: the page scrolls, as the controls page does today.

Pad: front ↓ settings, A → the first row → ↓↑ step every row in order, the headings skipped, the sheet scrolling to keep the focus in sight → ←→ on the volume → B to the front. (Optionally LB / RB jump to the next or last heading.) The controls table's rows are read, not pressed, so ↓ steps over them to back.

What the mock cannot show
  • The real font, the page-turn fade, or the pad's brackets moving: the corners here are drawn on one row, still.
  • Other languages: section names and tab names run longer in German or French; four tabs (B) on a phone are already at the small size and would wrap or need to scroll.
  • The landing page's column (index.html): it is narrower than the held sheet and stands at the left. The same sections would go there; the column has no touch or fullscreen row today, so its graphics and controls are a row shorter.
  • How the controls table grows when a pad has been seen (its pad column and pad-only rows are drawn here as if one has).
  • The iPhone's graphics section, where the fullscreen row is the one-line note instead.
  • A dev build's panel (backtick): it is not on the sheet and stays out of this.
Flags
  • Language fits none of the owner's three; it is drawn under a placeholder other. With "the two of you" gone it is the only row there on the pause sheet (plus feedback on the title page), so a section of one row -- the owner may prefer it under graphics, or a fourth word.
  • A and B change the sheet's height page to page (audio is two rows, controls a table), and the desk's sheet is centered, so it would re-center as you turn; holding one height for every section (the tallest) would need deciding at build.
  • Touch is sorted under controls (it changes how a press is taken), though what it changes is partly layout (the bottom sheet).
  • The held sheet today is a one-pixel edge with no shadow (see today-*.png); the mock follows the shot rather than a two-pixel edge and hard shadow.
