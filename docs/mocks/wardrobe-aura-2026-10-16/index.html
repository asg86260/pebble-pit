The two of you: auras, earned color, and locked items

Mock, not the game. Drawn from plain rects on canvas the way the wardrobe mock was (docs/mocks/pair-wardrobe-2026-10-16): a body is the 3 x 3 cell square (18 world pixels) with a 2-pixel rim; one cell is 6 world pixels; the particles are laid in 2-pixel units, the wardrobe's own WARD_UNIT, on a world-anchored 2-pixel grid. "4x" is four screen pixels to a world pixel; "game scale" is one to one. Every particle fades in and out by opacity; nothing appears or leaves at full strength. The canvases run on held frames, 12 a second.

The items already cut on wardrobe-cuts (pigtails, tiara, bowtie, tie, collar, medal, small pack, cat tail, bushy tail, grey, pale; black is "filled") are left out here.

Part 1: an aura slot

A sixth slot, aura, after pattern; "none" first, as every slot. Each tile: the bare body at 4x on the ground chosen above, then at game scale on the yard and in the deep. "Cost" is the most particles one body has alive at once and the rects it fills a frame; the live number under it is counted off this page's own draw. Only the two of you wear one, so the yard pays it twice: against the sky's 420 motes (AIR_CAP) every aura here is small change.

What I would keep, and the one I cut before drawing

  • Reads in a crowd: the rain cloud, the orbit, the embers, the footprints (walking only) and the scale swirl. Each is either a shape over the head or a thing that moves on its own clock round the body, which is what the eye finds at one to one.
  • Reads only close up: dust motes and petals at game scale are a few grey pixels; the yard already has dust and grains in the air, so they are found only if you know to look. Glints read as glints but are easy to miss between their rests.
  • Pebble bits read best walking (shaken off a step at a time) and look like a body dropping its load, which a carter really does: a false signal in the yard.
  • Smoke wisps were cut before drawing: grey rising off a body is what a machine's stack puts up (SMOG_TINTS.mach); on a square it would read as "this one is polluting".
  • Purple: only the embers use it, because they are the level-up's own embers, and the level-up is the deep's magic. On the yard they are still purple; whether that belongs up there is the owner's call. Every other aura is grey on the yard and the deep's turned-over grey below.
  • Name clash for the build: render/aura.js and AURA_BREATH / AURA_IN already exist (the offer flag's ring). The slot's code would want another file name, e.g. render/wardaura.js.

Part 2: colored items, earned

The owner (2026-10-16): some color for some of the appearance options, like a gold crown for beating the game, some aura for beating the game. Color is new to the wardrobe, so the colored items are only ever earned: nothing a fresh yard can wear has a hue. The yard's hues are already spoken for (blue the ore, green the crops, red the spark, purple the deep's magic, config/palette.js), so the earned color is gold, which nothing in the game uses yet. The item-to-achievement mapping is being collected on the decisions board; only the colored ones are named here.

A: one flat gold, reserved for earned items, nothing else in the game. B: a small earned palette, gold and a rose, still flat, one tone an item.

How each treatment reads

  • A, one gold: the strongest signal. In a black-and-white crowd the only warm pixel on the screen is the one who beat the game; it reads at one to one even as a single crown. It keeps the rule simple to say: gold means earned, and nothing else is gold. The cost: every colored item is the same gold, so a gold scarf and a gold crown say the same thing ("earned") and not which.
  • B, gold and a rose: more variety, but the second hue is where it starts to cost. Rose sits between the spark's red and the deep's purple and at game scale reads as one of them (a spark-colored petal, a magic-colored scarf). Any second hue has to dodge four taken ones; the only clean gaps left are gold and a pale cyan, and cyan sits on the ore's blue. If B is wanted, the cheaper second "color" is a silver: a bright pale grey with no hue at all.
  • Gold on the yard's white paper is the weakest pairing on the page: yellow has little contrast against white, so a gold crown is a lighter shape than the ink crown it replaces. The gold here is pulled toward ochre to keep it visible on white; in the deep, over black, it is lifted (as the coins are lifted on a dark page, COIN_DARK) and reads best of anywhere.
  • Flat, one tone an item: no shading, no glint highlights, no gradient. The halo breathes only by opacity, slowly, and never flashes.

Which achievement is "beating the game"

  • None of the existing ones. gotout ("you saved your sqouse") is the act-1 rescue, fired on S.rescued, hours before the end. allstations ("full party") is a deep milestone. The serpent ladder (levels 2, 5, 10, 25) reads S.serpentBest, the endless serpent's.
  • The moment the game is beaten is the finale: the serpent killed, the two of you walked home and into the house (src/finale.js), written down as S.retired. No notice fires there (src/notices.js has no rule on S.retired or S.finale).
  • A new notice is needed, one line in the catalog and one rule (() => S.retired), and a Steam twin comes free with it (src/achievements.js). Its name and note are the owner's: shown here as home.

Part 3: the locked look

The locks on the picker below are examples so the look can be seen; the real mapping is the decisions board's. Achievement names are the game's (locales/en.ftl); the lock line's wording is a placeholder. Unlocks would be the save's, shared by both squares.

The locked look, on the picker

Two ways to cycle past a locked item, side by side. In both: the item is drawn faint with a small lock, and the achievement's name shows under the row on hover or focus. Randomize never picks a locked item. The controls work: arrows, or up / down / left / right on the keyboard for the sheet under the pointer, r to randomize. Tick an achievement below to pretend it is earned.

A: skipped when cycling

  • Prev / next step over a locked item; the row never lands on one.
  • The count says how many are behind locks (the lock and the number); hovering or focusing the count lists them with their achievements.
  • Nothing to undo: the outfit can only ever hold what is open.

B: shown, but not kept

  • Prev / next land on a locked item: it is drawn on the body faint, as a try-on, with the lock and the achievement's name under the row.
  • Done keeps the last open item in that slot; a locked one is never saved.
  • Shows the player what there is to win, at the cost of a sheet that holds a thing it will not keep.

What this mock cannot show

  • The real yard: rocks, heaps, the sky's motes and grains, rain, carts and loads over the head. Dust motes and petals will be harder to find against the air than here, and a rain cloud sits where the weather's clouds and the speech marks are.
  • The deep's real palette: the deep is drawn as the yard and turned over, with its own capped grey ramp (render/deeptones.js). Here the deep is the yard's greys flipped by hand, and a faded particle over black is an alpha blend the deep's ramp may not hold.
  • The real zoom. The yard's zoom follows the window, and a 2-pixel unit at a fractional zoom is snapped to device pixels by the game's fill; at a far zoom a particle thins to one device pixel or doubles. Not tested.
  • The poses: crouched, carried, knocked flat, under the rock in act 1, the snatch and the finale. Each would need its aura held, hidden or let go; that is a build question.
  • Cost in the game: the counts here are rects per body per frame from this page, not a profile of render.js.
  • The real sheet: drawn by hand to look like the game's, not with its stylesheet; the phone layout is not drawn.
  • How an unlock is told: a notice, a mark on the settings row, nothing. Not drawn.
