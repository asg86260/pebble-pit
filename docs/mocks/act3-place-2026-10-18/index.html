Act 3: the place, at the yard's scale

A mock, drawn from plain shapes in a canvas. It is not the game: nothing here runs the sim or the game's renderer. It is drawn on the game's grid, P = 6. A body is 18 px square with a 2 px rim, and every stroke is 2 px. The colors are the game's eleven greys from palette.js. Every 1x canvas is one world pixel to one screen pixel, the way the yard draws. The 3x figures are crops of the same drawing scaled three times.

The rules held to: stations the size of the yard's (36 to 72 px), flat black outlines, grey only for material (heaps, loads, motes, mist), the yard's UI (the floating glyph, the counter, the pile-full mark, a board), and one heaven cue: the cloud floor. Nothing else glows, sparkles or wears a halo. The throne's glare belongs to God, not to the place.

Every word is a placeholder. Station names, captions on the canvas, board rows and prices are working names. Words with a dotted underline are placeholders, and the canvases carry no lettering of their own beyond the layout's labels, which are placeholders too.

1. The cloud floor

The owner: "the floor doesnt need to be flat, it should read like its in the clouds somehow". Each option shows a body walking and a saw mill with its sawdust heap (the grey is material), so you can check that feet land on something. The yard's own floor is a flat 2 px line over a 5 % hatch.

A. Billow edge recommended

The ground line itself goes lumpy: puffs one or two cells high, stepped on the grid, and pressed flat under every station. A body steps up and down a cell in held poses as it crosses them. Under the line, two fainter puff contours drift slowly, so the floor reads as layered cloud. They are lines, not speckle.

B. Flat walk, billows behind

The walking line stays the yard's flat line. Pale puffs rise a cell or two behind it and drift. Wisps drift through the slab, and its underside is scalloped, so you can see it floats. Feet read exactly as they do in the yard.

C. Thin patches

A gentle billow edge with gaps in it. Through a gap the yard shows far below in pale grey: its ground line, the rock and tiny bodies. The gap breathes open and shut by fade. Bodies keep to the solid cloud.

Why A: it is the only option where the floor's own line, the thing every body stands on, says "cloud". B keeps the yard's line and puts the cloud behind it, so it reads as a yard in front of a cloud. C is the most charming, but a gap is a hole a body must route round. A single thin patch from C could sit inside A as a set piece, where souls rise.

2. The camp layout

One whole strip, 2400 px wide, which is about the yard's width. It is drawn on floor A. The dashed frame is roughly what the camera shows at once, with the counter in its corner. Stations are placeholder boxes at their real sizes. Members walk out of God's yard to the camp's waiting ground, and the tithe line climbs the stair into the glare.

A. Camp left, crowd right recommended

Left to right: grove, saw mill, chipper, paper mill, workshop, press, construction shack, pedestal (the pair face right), then God's yard and the crowd, then the stair and the throne. The chain runs one way, from tree to sign, and ends at the pedestal, which faces the crowd. Members walk left out of the crowd to the waiting ground by the shack. The camera opens on the pedestal and the front of the crowd.

B. Crowd in the middle

The grove and the wood end of the chain (saw mill, chipper, paper mill) stand left. The crowd is in the middle. The pedestal (the pair face left), the shack, the workshop and the press stand right, between the crowd and the stair, so the tithe line walks through the union's camp to reach the stair. A good picture. But timber and paper have to cross the crowd to the workshop and press, and the camp is cut in two.

3. The pedestal and the pair

Each look is drawn twice: left idle, right mid-speech. A line of the speech is a held pose: a crouch, then up a cell with the shout mark. A bubble rises and fades. Rebel knocks off as grey motes onto the pile at the pedestal's foot, and each mote fades as it lands.

A. Plinth

A stepped stone block, two courses. Civic, and read at once as "a place to speak from". Nothing about it says union.

B. Soapbox recommended

A crate, its planks in grey (material). It is the agitator's own furniture: the pair stand on a box at the crowd's edge. It is low and plain, the yard's size, and the bodies on it are the thing you see.

C. Cloud pillar

A lump of the floor pushed up, billow-topped like floor A. Most "heaven", but it spends the heaven cue on furniture and reads as part of the ground rather than as something the union put there.

4. The construction shack

Where you order buildings. Each look stands at its door with a member. Over it floats the glyph of the order in hand, filling a cell at a time, the way the yard's stack does. To the right, the ordered station rises in bands, each band fading in, while a member carries timber (grey) to it. The board beside the options is the yard's sheet, in plain CSS.

A. Hut with a plan board recommended

A small hut with a board on two posts beside it, three little station outlines pinned to the board. "Plans on a board" reads as "pick one".

B. Scaffold and hoist

An open frame of poles with a jib and a block on a rope that swings in held steps, with a timber stack at its foot. It reads as "building" more than "ordering", and looks like a site still going up.

C. Foreman's tent

A peaked tent with a trestle and a rolled plan. Fits a camp that is still a camp, but at 1x the tent and the trestle read as one triangle.

5. The crowd and God's yard

The crowd at yard scale. Pale souls have grey rims and members have ink rims; a soul's rim darkens to ink as it is won, and then it walks out toward the camp (left). Shown in each look: a sign planted among the crowd, posters on a standing board, and a warden, a taller body with a solid cap, told apart by shape, not by tone. A new pale soul fades in at the back to keep the loop closed. There is no speckled field anywhere.

A. Terraces

Three ranks, each on its own billow terrace, the back ranks higher. It reads most like a crowd. But it is three floors, the yard has one, and bodies on the back terraces stand where nothing else in the game stands.

B. One file, a bank behind recommended

The yard's rule: everyone on the one floor, shoulder to shoulder. Behind them, a cloud bank holds one faint row of rims in the lightest grey, only to say "more of them". God's yard empties from the front as members walk out. Quietest at 1x.

C. Work gangs

The crowd at work: souls hauling mist from God's heap to the tithe pile, in loose gangs. It is the liveliest look and the most like the yard, but a crowd that walks about is hard to count, and the empty share is harder to see.

6. The throne and the stair

God is a placeholder: a big square with a crown. The glare is God's, so it is the one light thing in the act, and it pulses slowly by fade. The tithe line climbs the stair a step at a time, in held poses, each carrier with a grey bundle. At the top each one walks into the glare and fades out, and a new one fades in at the stair's foot.

A. Straight stair, ray glare recommended

A plain flight of cell steps, two cells to a step, outlined in ink like a yard ramp, rising to a platform. The glare is twelve short rays round the throne in pale grey, long and short in turn. Simple, and it reads at a glance as "up there".

B. Cloud switchback, band glare

The stair is three billow ledges in a zigzag, and the glare is horizontal bands behind the throne, like a sunrise. Softer and more heavenly, but it spends the cloud cue twice, and the carriers' path is harder to follow.

What the mock cannot show

  • The game's renderer. These are plain shapes on a canvas, drawn on its grid and in its greys. The game's sprites, its ink map and its frame-rate behavior are not here.
  • The camera. The layout is a still strip with a frame on it. How the view pans, opens and follows is not drawn.
  • A crowd of thousands. A few dozen souls are drawn. The fact that only the files in view would be drawn is described, not shown.
  • The sim. Nobody here decides anything: walks, wins and the tithe line are loops on a clock, sped up so they close.
  • The boards' real builder and stylesheet. The one board is plain CSS.
  • Sound, the pointer, and the click on the pair (the speech here plays on a timer).
  • The yard's own scene beside it. The floor and stations are matched by eye to shots of crew, after-camp and after-chain, not measured against a running build.
  • God, the throne, the stations' real faces, the speech's words: all placeholders, each waiting on its own mock-vote.
