Act 3: God's yard as a huge crowd

A mock, drawn from plain shapes in a canvas. It is not the game: no sim, no game renderer. It uses the game's grid (P = 6), its eleven greys and the dark page's lightness map. Each 1x canvas is 133 by 100 cells (798 by 600 px), about what the yard's camera shows, with one world pixel to one screen pixel. The 3x figures are crops of the same frame. The floor is floor A (the billow edge), the layout is layout A (camp left, crowd, then throne) and the pedestal is C (the cloud pillar), as chosen.

Every option tells the same story. A sign and a poster stand in the front of the crowd and ink the souls round them. A soul whose rim closes becomes a member (an ink rim) and walks out left to the camp, leaving a hole. A pale soul fades in to refill each hole. A warden (taller, solid cap) paces the front, and the tithe line climbs to the throne at the far right. Only the front row is real bodies. Everything behind it is a texture drawn from counts: each soul is a rank, and the share slider sets the counts. Signs and posters raise the share near themselves and speed up the turnover there, so the ink gathers round them.

Every word is a placeholder, including the slider's name and every caption. The throne is a placeholder too.

1. Rows receding recommended

The front row is full size: 18 px bodies. Each row behind sits a little higher, smaller and paler: 12, 9 and 6 px bodies with 2 px rims, then solid dots of 4 px and of 2 px, twenty rows up to a horizon contour. A member is ink at every depth, so the spread of support reads at a glance, and a soul who walks out leaves a hole in the far rows too (it fades out, as nothing can be seen walking there).

2. Parallax

The same rows as option 1. Each scrolls at its own rate as the camera pans: the front row, the camp and the throne at the world's rate, the far rows slower, down to a twenty-fifth at the horizon. The pan is automatic here, 60 px each way over 16 seconds, so it shows. Each row moves in whole pixels, so the far rows move in steps.

3. Background crowd

No floor horizon. The crowd rises behind everything, tier on tier up into the sky, like stadium seating seen from the pitch, smaller toward the top and widening as it climbs. The throne sits on a plinth on top of it, and the tithe line climbs a steep stair up its right side.

Calls made in the mock

  • The far dots are 2 px at the smallest, not whole cells. The rows go 18, 12, 9, 6 px (each with a 2 px rim), then solid 4 px dots, then solid 2 px dots. Whole-cell dots would stop at 6 px, which is the 6 px row already, and the crowd would end about four rows sooner. Nothing is drawn 1 px wide; the page checks this on its own canvases (see the numbers under each option).
  • The back rows' baselines are on whole pixels, not on the 6 px grid. A 9 px or a 4 px body cannot sit on the grid. The front row, the floor, the camp, the stair and the throne are all on the grid.
  • The share is split 70 / 30. At a share won of s, about 0.7 s of the crowd's places are holes (walked out) and 0.3 s are members still in the crowd (inked, waiting their turn to leave). So 100 % reads as mostly empty with ink in what is left, and 0 % is all pale. A sign or a poster adds up to 35 points of share near itself and makes its neighbors turn over up to eight times as fast.
  • A walker fades out as it reaches the camp, where two members already stand. In the game it would join the waiting ground.
  • Pale souls vary between two or three greys within a row (the per-cell rule), and the default grey gets lighter row by row. Members are always full ink.
  • One warden is drawn in the front row (pacing) and one small warden stands in the 9 px row, to show a warden can be read at depth by its cap.

What the mock cannot show

  • The frame cost in the real renderer. The number under each option is this page's own canvas time for that frame, on this machine, in a browser with nothing else running. The game draws its world through its own layers, ink map and camera. A crowd of a few thousand souls drawn as rects every frame could cost real milliseconds there. It probably wants each back row cached as a bitmap and redrawn only when a soul changes state. Nobody has measured that. For scale: in a headless browser here, this page drew one option's frame in about 1 to 4 ms, option 3 the dearest. That is a canvas with nothing else on it, not the game.
  • Thousands. Options 1 and 2 draw about 2,000 places and option 3 about 2,500. A crowd of ten thousand would need either a smaller dot than 2 px (which breaks the hairline rule) or rows off camera. How the act's counts map onto drawn places is not decided.
  • The sim. Wins, walk-outs and refills are a loop on a clock, driven by a rank for each place, sped up so they close. Nobody chooses anything. Moving the slider down can make a walker vanish partway, because the loop is a clock and not a log.
  • The camera. Option 2 pans on its own. How the real camera opens, pans and follows is not drawn, and the other two options do not pan at all.
  • The rest of the place. The grove, the mills and most of the camp are off the left edge. Only the pedestal and the waiting ground show.
  • The game's sprites, its smog and weather, sound, and the pointer. The speech on the pedestal plays on a timer.
  • The throne, God, the sign and the poster are placeholders, each waiting on its own mock and vote.
