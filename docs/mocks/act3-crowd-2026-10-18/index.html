Act 3: God's yard as a huge crowd, smaller and smaller

A mock, drawn from plain shapes in a canvas. It is not the game: no sim, no game renderer. It uses the game's grid (P = 6), its eleven greys and the dark page's lightness map. Each 1x canvas is 133 by 100 cells (798 by 600 px), about the yard camera's width, one world pixel to one screen pixel. The 3x figures are crops of the same frame. It is drawn on floor A, layout A and pedestal C, as chosen.

The owner on the first round: "crowd still needs work. i think the crowd should get smaller and smaller." Options (a) to (c) read that as shrinking into the distance: row after row, each a little smaller than the one before it and a little closer to it, from the 18 px front row down to single dots at a horizon. Option (d) is an alternate reading: the crowd itself gets smaller as the union wins. The first round's parallax and background-crowd options are dropped from this page; they are still in git history (commit 05e03d30).

All four tell the same story. A sign and a poster in the front row ink the souls round them. A soul whose rim closes becomes a member (an ink rim) and walks out left to the camp, leaving a hole. A pale soul fades in to refill the hole. A warden (taller, with a solid cap) paces the front, and the tithe line climbs to the placeholder throne on the right. Only the front row is real bodies; the rows behind it are a texture drawn from counts. Every word is a placeholder.

(a) Linear shrink

Each row is one pixel smaller than the row in front: 17, 16, 15 and so on down to 1 px, then three more rows of single dots. The gap between rows shrinks with the size. It is even and orderly, a slope rather than a distance: the crowd reads as a bank tilted away from you more than as a plain going far off.

(b) Perspective shrink recommended

Size falls off as one over distance: 18, 14, 11, 9, 8, 7, 6, 6, 5 near the front, then long runs of 4, 3 and 2 px rows, then single dots. The near rows step down fast and the far ones crowd together, so the eye reads depth: a few rows of people, then a field of them, then a horizon. Members are ink at every depth.

(c) Perspective shrink, far rows into the cloud

The rows of (b), and past the first quarter of the depth each row's greys step toward the cloud floor's own pale grey. Members fade from ink to light grey and pale souls to the faintest grey, so the crowd dissolves into the cloud instead of ending at a line. It is the most distant-looking, but far members are no longer ink, so the share won is harder to read in the back half.

(d) Alternate reading: the crowd shrinks as the union wins

A different reading of the owner's words: "smaller and smaller" as the act goes on, not into the distance. The rows of (b), but the crowd's depth is the share still in God's yard. As members walk out, the back row fades away, then the next, so the crowd draws in toward the front. Past 70 % the front row thins too, until God's yard is a handful of souls round the sign and the poster. A hole refills sooner here, because the crowd closes up rather than going gappy. Drag the slider to see it.

Calls made in the mock

  • The last rows are single pixels. The brief named them, so they are drawn, 1 px each on a 3 px pitch, as the last four rows only. Everything larger is 2 px or more: bodies of 6 px and over are hollow with a 2 px rim, and bodies of 5 px and under are solid squares, so no shape has a 1 px stroke or a 1 px hole. The page counts 1 px runs on each frame, outside the single-dot rows and inside them, under each option.
  • Rows do not overlap. The gap between rows is a quarter of the row's size (at least 1 px), and the gap within a row a third (at least 2 px for the solid ones). Each shape stays clean at 1x. Overlapping rows, heads peeking over heads, would pack more in but turn the far rows to grit.
  • Bodies below 18 px are off the 6 px grid (allowed by the brief), all on whole pixels. The front row, the floor, the camp, the stair and the throne are on the grid.
  • Depth is measured as distance, not as row count (18 ÷ size − 1), so a sign's reach is the same in every option: about two rows of 9 px behind it. The second sign stands in the 11 px row, and a small capped warden in the 9 px row.
  • The share is split 70 / 30 in (a) to (c): at a share won of s, about 0.7 s of the places are holes and 0.3 s are members still waiting in the crowd. In (d) the split is 15 / 30, because there the share mostly shows as missing rows.
  • In (c) the greys step by whole palette steps, row by row: no alpha and no gradient. Members keep full ink for the first quarter of the depth.
  • A walker fades out as it reaches the camp, where two members already wait.

What the mock cannot show

  • The frame cost in the real renderer. The number under each option is this page's own canvas time for that frame, in a browser with nothing else on the page. The game draws through its own layers, ink map and camera. A few thousand rects every frame could cost real milliseconds there, and it probably wants each back row cached as a bitmap and redrawn only when a place changes state. Nobody has measured that.
  • Thousands. These draw about 1,400 places in (a) and 3,800 in (b) and (c). A bigger crowd than that needs more single-dot rows or rows off camera. How the act's counts map onto drawn places is not decided.
  • The sim. Wins, walk-outs and refills are a loop on a clock, a sliding rank for each place, sped up so they close. Nobody chooses anything. Dragging the slider down can make a walker vanish partway.
  • The camera, and what is off the left edge: the grove, the mills and most of the camp.
  • The game's sprites, smog, weather and sound, and the pointer. The speech on the pedestal plays on a timer.
  • The throne, God, the sign and the poster are placeholders, each waiting on its own mock and vote.
