Act 3: God's yard, round 3: a parallax crowd, a far-off God, and the zoom

A mock, drawn from plain shapes in a canvas. It is not the game: no sim, no game renderer. It uses the game's grid (P = 6), its eleven greys and the dark page's lightness map. The canvas is 133 by 100 cells (798 by 600 px), about the yard camera's width, one world pixel to one screen pixel when not zoomed. It is drawn on floor A, with layout A and pedestal C, as chosen.

The owner, on round 1: "lets have god be in the distance as well, but still large, and when scrolling over through the crowd, we focus in on the top of the throne, in a zoom type fashion. i like the parallax crowd, but the crowd should blend together in the distance, less individual squares and more of a large mass". Round 2's smooth shrink is kept for the near rows. Round 1's options are in commit 05e03d30, and round 2's in 2cc873c6.

The story is unchanged. A sign and a poster (two of each now, across a longer crowd) ink the souls round them. A soul whose rim closes walks out left to the camp, leaving a hole, and a pale soul fades in to refill it. A warden paces the front, and the tithe carriers climb the cloud switchback to God. Only the front row is real bodies. Every word is a placeholder, and so are God and the throne.

The crowd through to God main option

  • Parallax. Eleven layers pan at their own rates. The front row, the camp and the floor move at the world's rate. The near rows go from 0.95 down to 0.62, the three mass bands at 0.55, 0.42 and 0.3, and God and the horizon at 0.2.
  • The near rows are round 2's perspective shrink for nine rows: 14, 11, 9, 8, 7, 6, 6, 5 and 5 px bodies behind the 18 px front row. Each is still a soul that inks, leaves and refills.
  • The mass. Past those rows nothing is a separate soul. It is three bands, each a flat silhouette of grey cells: 4 px, then 3 px, then 2 px cells, palest furthest. Each band has a lumpy top edge of heads (a cell on most columns, at random, over a slow rise and fall) and one grey with a sixth of its cells a step lighter or darker. Members show as ink clumps drifting slowly through it, more as the share rises. Walk-outs show as dips in the band's top edge, deeper and wider as the share rises.
  • God sits on the far horizon, on the slowest layer: a throne taller than the whole crowd, God the big square with a crown, the glare behind, and the cloud switchback (three billow ledges and flights) leading up to the seat with tiny carriers on it. Far off, God and the throne are drawn in a mid grey, hazed. The ink version fades in over them as you arrive.
  • The zoom. The pan moves evenly, and the zoom stays near 1x through the crowd (1.17x at the middle), then dives to 2.4x at the far end. The top of the throne is drawn to the middle of the frame, so the arrival is God's face filling the view. Every edge is rounded after the zoom, so shapes stay whole pixels.

Secondary note: the alternate reading from round 2

"Smaller and smaller" read as the act goes on: the crowd's back rows fade away as members walk out, until God's yard is a handful of souls round the sign and the poster. It could sit on top of the main option: the mass bands would thin and sink as the share rises.

Calls made in the mock

  • The zoom is one camera zoom on every layer, with parallax only across. A true move forward would grow the near layers faster than the far ones. That pushes the crowd off the bottom sooner and makes God grow last, which reads as less of a zoom on the throne.
  • The zoom curve is the cube of an ease: 1.0x at the camp, 1.17x mid-crowd, 2.4x at God. A steady zoom loses the floor and the front row before the middle of the crowd.
  • Whole pixels, not whole cells, while zoomed. Each shape's two edges are rounded after the zoom. So a cell is 6 px at 1x and 14 or 15 px at 2.4x, varying by a pixel, and no stroke ever drops under 2 px. Snapping every cell to an exact scaled size would make the zoom move in visible steps.
  • God's haze is a crossfade, a mid-grey drawing under an ink one whose opacity rises with the pan, so nothing steps.
  • The mass has no individual walk-outs. The share shows as ink clumps (members) and dips in the top edge (souls gone). In the near rows and the front row each soul still inks, leaves and refills.
  • The front layer is about 1,320 px wide, the crowd's length, with two signs and two posters along it. Walkers walk to the camp at 40 px a second, so a member from the far end takes about half a minute.
  • The front-layer stair and throne from rounds 1 and 2 are gone. The tithe line climbs God's switchback, far off, as tiny 6 px carriers.

What the mock cannot show

  • The frame cost in the real renderer. The number under the canvas is this page's own draw time, in a browser with nothing else on the page: about 2 to 4 ms here, most of it the mass's few thousand cells. The game draws through its own layers, ink map and camera, and zoom is new to it. Caching each band as a bitmap that is redrawn only when its counts change is the likely shape. Nobody has measured that.
  • The real camera. The pan here runs on a clock or a slider. How the player moves the view, whether the zoom is tied to the pan or to a key or the wheel, and how the camera returns are not decided.
  • What the player does when zoomed in on God. At 2.4x the camp and the crowd are off screen.
  • The sim. Wins, walk-outs and refills are a loop on a clock, sped up so they close. The mass's clumps and dips are noise fields shaped by the share, not counts of anyone. Dragging the share down can make a walker vanish partway.
  • The grove, the mills and most of the camp, off the left edge. Also the game's sprites, smog, weather and sound.
  • God, the throne, the sign and the poster are placeholders, each waiting on its own mock and vote.
