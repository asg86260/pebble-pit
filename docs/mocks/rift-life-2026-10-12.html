Life Through the Rift

The pit's serpent glimpses, given to the rift: things crossing the sky on the other side of the tear, and now and then the serpent itself. Every pane shows the rift at both ends of its life on one clock: just torn, 7 cells on the left and grown, 20 cells on the right, so each glimpse can be judged at the size it will be seen most (small, for hours) and at its best (big, just before the drowning). Drawn on the 6px grid in the rift's look: the wavering rim, the paper halo, the three parallax skies of purple stars, the deep's eight creatures in the deep's own tones.

This is a mock. It is drawn from plain shapes, not by the game. It cannot show: the camera, so the three star skies and the creatures do not slide against each other as the yard pans (in the game that is where most of the depth comes from); the lens bend on the yard round the rim; the real grain stream and its orbits (a handful of stand-ins circle here); the rift growing between the two sizes; the zoom; or the game's frame pacing. Time is squeezed: each pane plays two or three glimpses a minute so you are not waiting, where the game would wait the randomized interval written under each one. The creatures here are not bent by the lens the way the stars are (see the open questions).

Shared to all of them

  • Only while torn: between the tear and the drowning. Never during the tear's gulp or any cutscene, never two at once, and the first one no sooner than a minute after the tear, so the tear itself stands alone. A yard reaching the rescue first gets only the eight-second swell before the drowning: nothing is scheduled into it.
  • Behind the rim, between the skies: everything is drawn inside the tear's clip, between the star layers by depth: a far thing has the middle and near stars over it, a near one is over all of them. The grains going in stay over everything.
  • Held steps: every crossing moves a whole cell at a time on its depth's beat (far 2.5, middle 3.5, near 5 cells a second); the creatures' own wriggles are their deep sprites at an eighth of a second. No flash, no shake, nothing brightens the disc.
  • The same world behind both sizes: a creature or the serpent is the same size and speed whatever the disc measures, so the small rift is a keyhole and the grown one a window.
  • Picture only: no hit area, no board, no sound (the hits-only rule).

Open questions

  • Foreshadowing only. The rift closes at the drowning and the serpent is first met at the snatch, after it, so every serpent glimpse through the rift comes before the player has seen him. Is that the point (a promise), or should the rift show only creatures and leave the serpent to the pit?
  • Should the lens bend them? The stars are pushed out toward the rim. A creature bent the same way would stretch as it nears the edge and squeeze through the middle, which reads as lensing but breaks the sprites into gaps at 7 cells.
  • Small-rift sizes. Near creatures and the eye are bigger than the just-torn window on purpose. If you would rather the 7-cell rift always show a whole creature, the near depth is dropped until the disc reaches about 12 cells.
