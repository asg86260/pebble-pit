Moving between stations: hops or a free cursor

Both shown on the same yard, reaching the same four targets: three stations and a roster's plus sign. The free cursor is a hand on the left stick with acceleration, the way console ports of mouse games usually do it.

Recommended: hops, with the free cursor kept as the hand on RT for the three things that need a place rather than a target: sweeping dust, throwing it, and dropping a body somewhere that is not a station. The yard is a line, so a hop is one press a station; every click in the game already lands on a named thing with a rectangle (stations.js, the rosters, the levers, the coil), so the hop list is read off tables that exist. A free cursor makes every press an aim, and the small targets (a roster's plus is three cells) are where a stick cursor is worst.

The camera: the focus pulls it, the right stick pushes it

  • Focus following. Each hop sends the view with the glide the hop arrows and the digit keys already use (lookAt): the camera eases there, slower than the marker, and never cuts. Under reduced motion it moves the way lookAt moves there today.
  • Stick pan. The right stick slides the view at the arrow keys' speed scaled by how far it is pushed, and the follow lets go, the way a drag lets go of it today. The focus stays on its station; if that station leaves the window, a small accent chevron at the window's edge points back at it.
  • Back to the focus. R click glides the view back. The next hop also brings it back: a hop from a station that is off screen goes to the station nearest the middle of the window instead, so the first press after a pan lands where you are looking.

On-screen glyphs

Tips, mouse and pad together, and the phone

  • Tips on focus. The hover tip (showTip / showTipAt) is asked for by the focused thing's rectangle instead of the cursor, after the same dwell, and goes when the focus moves. A board card's tip sits beside the card, on whichever side has room. Nothing new is written: the tip says what the hover says.
  • Last input wins. Mouse and pad are both always live. The first pad press shows the marker where the focus was and turns the prompts to pad glyphs; the first mouse move hides the marker and turns them back. Nothing has to be switched in settings, and the Deck's right trackpad (a mouse) and its sticks (a pad) can be used in the same minute.
  • The phone. Touch is untouched. The phone's hop arrows (hop.js) and the pad's d-pad walk the same list of stops in the same order, so the two are one mechanism with two front ends, and the digit keys' stops are that list too. A Deck's touch screen is a touch, and plays as the phone does. The pad's crew-sight hop (LT and the d-pad over bodies) is a shape a phone could later borrow for the crew sight it does not have.
