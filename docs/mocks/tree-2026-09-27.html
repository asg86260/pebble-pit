Every one is live: class, branch and capstone are free clicks, the buy button lights the next pip, and every node says what it does on hover or tap.

What this mock cannot show

  • It is drawn from plain shapes, not the game: no board.js, no real tierRows, no shelf builder, no dev-panel knobs.
  • The numbers on rungs 1-3 and 5-7 are placeholders; only the keystones and capstones are serpent-classes.md's words. The bills are a made-up deep ladder in the right band shape.
  • No purse: every rung is affordable, so the dashed "short of a coin" tag never shows.
  • The fighter is a stand-in drawing, still; nobody swims, nothing is built, the altar has no dome.
  • One station. A deep with five kinds, some built twice, four fighters, is not drawn: how the player gets between trees is not answered here.
  • The board's real width, the sheet's gutters, and how it sits over the deep's dark floor.

The marks, in all three

The pip's color is its band, as on every ladder: rungs 1-4 the ink of the scale, 5-6 the quarry's blue, 7-8 the spark's red. The station's depth is one number: switching branch or class keeps it, and lights the same depth on the new path.

1 · Columns

Bargain: the whole tree as one picture, the fork and every path readable at a glance, and the effects only in a tip; it costs the most room and a second vocabulary beside the boards' cards, and at 400 px its six columns are 58 px each with 8 px type, readable but the tightest of the three.

2 · Rails

Bargain: six rows of pips that read left to right like the ladders do, and the effects always written out in the panel, not a hover; it costs the fork's picture (the classes are two headings, not a split), and on a phone the panel drops under the rails, so the effect is a glance down rather than beside.

2b · Rails, collapsing

Bargain: once you choose, the rails fold down to the one path you took, so the board says what this fighter is and is the shortest of all at 400 px; it costs the standing menu of the paths not taken, which only Reset tree brings back (it refunds every rung and starts the tree blank), and a few frames of fold to build.

3 · Tree on the board

Bargain: the board stays the board, its cards unchanged, and a thin tree strip on top shows every path; the smallest change and the cheapest to build, but the strip's pips are tiny and its effects are a one-line caption, and at 400 px the cards fall three to a plank under a strip that still fits.
