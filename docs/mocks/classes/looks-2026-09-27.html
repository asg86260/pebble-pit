Two directions for telling ten classes apart without a hat, each drawn for all ten, so the vote is on a direction, not a sprite. Directions 2 (silhouette) and 3 (little figures) were dropped before drawing, on the owner's word.

  • 1 · Kit first. Costs: a fighter is wider than its square (up to nine cells), every attack has to move its kit, and a class read from across the deep rests on one or two grey pieces. Buys: the class is known from its outline at the game's scale, the blow comes out of the thing it is struck with (the bow shoots, the staff casts), and it stays the square on the P grid.
  • 4 · Square plus emblem. Costs: it is the one direction that leaves the P grid (the emblem is drawn in two-pixel units, a third of a cell, the way the fight numbers are drawn in screen pixels); a 14-pixel emblem is read when looked at, not at a glance; any dark mark on a white square is read as a face first; and the outline says nothing, so ten fighters in a row are ten squares. Buys: the square untouched and every fighter the same footprint, nothing to animate but the attack, and a set of ten icons the boards and cards can use as they are.
