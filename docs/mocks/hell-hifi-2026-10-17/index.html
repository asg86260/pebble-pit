Act 4: hell, high fidelity

A mock drawn from plain cells at the game's sizes (P = 6, a body 18 with a 2-pixel rim), not the game. Design: DESIGN.md, "Act 4: hell, the mine and the forge (design, not built)". Hell is drawn dark on both pages; the page around it follows the theme. Every frame shows at game scale or at 3x (pixelated, nothing smoothed).

Placeholders. Nothing in a picture has words. Every player-facing word on this page (the board in section 4) is a placeholder, dash-underlined like this. Every ore, bar, tool, station and barrier name in a caption is a working name from DESIGN.md, marked like this. The owner writes the real words.

How it reads as hell in black and white. Five things carry the heat with no red in them. (1) Light from below: the floors burn, so the undersides of everything catch the light, rock lightens toward the floor a rung at a time, and a body's bottom rim is white. The yard lights things from above, so this alone reads as wrong-way-up. (2) White fire: flames are held white shapes stepping down the deep's greys at the edges, redrawn every 110 ms rather than tweened. (3) Glare: rock and air around a fire are dithered up the ramp per cell, flickering by the rung, never by opacity. (4) Shimmer: above every fire, rows of cells are shifted a whole cell left or right in held steps, so the haze stays on the P grid. (5) Embers: single cells rise from every fire, white cooling to the greys, fading out by opacity. Purple shows up in exactly three places, the three that are magic: the road's portal, God's halo, and God's light going into the souls when their lives come back. Red shows up once, on the board, for the spark band.

1. The run of chambers, establishing view

A cutaway of all five chambers at once. The game's camera holds one chamber at a time, so this is the view the design describes but never shows whole. Left to right and down: the landing (the road's portal in the roof, the muster's ladder, the anvil and smith, the quench tank, a furnace, a dross seam on the left wall), then chambers 2 to 4 (cinder iron, pitch copper, obsidian glass), then the cage room (adamant), with God hanging in the cage and its glow lighting the room. The deep's floor is the purple-edged band across the top; the road comes down the shaft to the portal. The slider sets how far through the act it is. At 2, rubble and the bone wall are down and lie in spoil heaps, the first stair is cut, builders are cutting the second a flight at a time, and a wall gang is working the lava gate. The chambers past it are unlit: nobody is down there and nothing is built, but their floors still burn. A flare goes off in the deepest open chamber about every nine seconds.

2. The stations, at 3x, idle and working

The ore faces, one for each tier, each with its own grain

A face is a block of cells in the left wall. The grain is how you tell the five apart, and each barrier carries the grain of the seam that matches it (below). Dross: soft mottled slag in mid greys, no highlight. Cinder iron: dark rock in bands, with flecks still hot that twinkle. Pitch copper: pitch black with bright veins that wind through it and catch a sheen. Obsidian glass: black facets with pale edges, and a glint that sweeps across them. Adamant: a bright regular lattice, the only seam that looks made, with a slow shine. Working: a ghost miner swings in held poses (rest, wind-up held, smear, impact held, follow-through). Chips fly on every blow, every third blow a counted lump drops, and a hauler carries it off. The face is already one cell deep where it has been worked.

The furnace: cold, lit, stoked, out of fuel

A squat stone furnace with a hopper on top, an arched door, a flue into the roof, a brimstone bin on the left and a bar tray on the right. Cold: door black, the stone dark. Lit: fire in the door, the stone around it lit, the stoker standing by. Stoked: the stoker shovels brimstone in held throws, the core goes white, the glare reaches the walls, embers rise from the door and the flue, and the air above shimmers. Out of fuel: the bin is empty, the fire sinks to greys with no white left, the flue smokes, ore heaps up over the hopper and the pile-full mark comes up.

Brimstone: the crust round a vent

A vent in the floor puffs and licks white. Crust grows round it a crystal at a time, each fading in. A gatherer walks over, knocks the crust off a crystal a blow, and carries a lump away. Every crystal grows back, so the loop starts empty again with nothing popping.

The anvil and the smith

Idle: an empty anvil, bars and the old pick waiting on the tray. Working: a hauler brings the bars in; the smith works the blank in held strokes (raise held, smear, impact held with a burst of white sparks that cool as they fall, recover); the pips above count the strokes (TOOL_STROKES, 12 here); the blank cools a rung every few strokes; the finished pick is carried out.

The quench: black water from the abyss

For the top two tools only. A stone tank of black water with a pale surface line. A ghost brings a bucket down from the deep and pours it in. The smith holds the white-hot glass-edged pick over the tank, dips it in two held steps, steam bursts and cools up the greys, the surface boils, and the pick comes out dark with a white edge.

The barriers, each worked by a wall gang

Each barrier is a body of cells filling the passage. The gang's leader swings at the face with the tool it yields to; the next member waits behind (the size of the gang is a choice on the card); haulers carry every loose piece to the spoil heap, which grows a cell at a time. The wall is undercut: a column at a time from the face, bottom to top, each piece falling as it comes loose, so a half-broken wall is a wall with a hole in it. Each barrier carries its matching seam's grain. Rubble: dross slag in fused lumps, with gaps that show the glow. The bone wall: courses of pale bone with skulls among them, mortared in banded cinder with hot flecks. The lava gate: cast black plates veined like pitch copper, riveted, fire showing white through the seams; once it is breached, fire runs out along the floor and cools a cell at a time, darkening to crust. The anchor chains: strands of obsidian links, cut from the bottom a link at a time. The cage: adamant bars round God, cut a segment at a time. Each loop is 24 seconds, ending in a fade (a mock loop, not a game event). Under each animation is the breakdown as a strip of stills at game scale.

A failed attempt: the tool rings off

The pair at the rubble with the yard's pick. A blow rings off: white rings open at the point of contact, a chip comes off the pick, and nothing comes loose. A second blow does the same. Then the camera glides across the chamber (the abyss's glide) to the dross face, whose grain is the rubble's grain, and corner brackets fade in round it. Then it glides back. The pair can be dressed: these two wear the default outfits, drawn in the soul's pale inks.

3. Assets sheet

Each asset at 3x. Lumps are counted items, 7 by 6 two-pixel units. Bars are 9 by 4. All names are working names.

The flare: the warning, then the flare

The vent's glow builds in held rings (the serpent's phase-warning register), and the miner steps back. Then a column of white fire goes up to the roof, embers burst and the air shimmers. The face it reaches crusts over with slag, hot spots cooling, and brimstone crusts round the vent. The miner comes back and clears the crust a blow a cell before ore comes off again.

Freeing God, a link at a time

The cage's front bars are cut. God hangs inside on two chains. The links come off one at a time from God's end, alternating sides, and each falls. God drops in two held steps, lands, and walks out. The halo brightens as each link goes. What God looks like is a placeholder (a large living square: black rim, white middle) and the owner's call.

Lives returned

In God's glare, a mote of God's light (purple: magic) travels to each soul's rim. Each of the twelve pieces goes from pale to black as one arrives, a piece at a time, alternating between the two. Then the middles fill to white and the outfits turn to the yard's ink: bodies again. The rims closing are the line.

4. The forge's board

The board copies the shape of the game's sheet (two cards across, the goal card in a double rule, section signs inked the other way round, pips top right, bill on the third line, dashed when a coin is short). Ladders are eight rungs in four bands through tierRows: brimstone only, then brimstone and low bars, then brimstone and low and high bars, then low and high bars and a spark. Hell's coins have no color, so the pips of the first three bands are all ink and only the spark band is red. Open: whether each band's pips should take its coin's shape instead. The coin marks are drawn the way the world draws the items: brimstone is a crystal, and each bar has its own fill (dross dotted, iron solid, copper banded, glass hollow, adamant latticed). The barrier card shows the wall's grain beside the seam that matches it. Every word is a placeholder.

What this mock cannot show

  • The game's renderer: the deep's inverted-palette pipeline (hell here is drawn straight as seen), device-pixel snapping, the wardrobe's real outfits, the class kits. The pair's outfits here are rough stand-ins.
  • The camera: the game holds one chamber in view, with the next barrier at its right edge. The establishing view is a cutaway no player sees. Walking back up the run with the view's arrows is not drawn.
  • Pathing: haulers ping-pong on straight lines. The real route up the stair, through each passage and to the anvil (route.js climbs) is not simulated. Ghosts pass through each other here.
  • The cost of shimmer: here it is a strip copy per cell row at blit time. Whether the game can afford it in four running places is a PERF.md question.
  • Any numbers: blows per barrier, the burn, batch sizes, stroke counts and pacing are made up to make the loops read.
  • The loops' resets: each vignette fades out and back in at the end of its loop. That is the mock repeating, not a game event. In the game a face only deepens and a heap only grows.
  • Satan (call 4 is open), the muster's card, the carriers on the road, the beats (wall-first, wall-<n>, unchain, alive) as cutscenes, and sound.
  • Reading on a real screen at the game's zoom, and on a phone.
  • Every word: none in the pictures, placeholders on the board.
