Act 4: hell, high fidelity (round 2)

A mock drawn from plain cells at the game's sizes (P = 6, a body 18 with a 2-pixel rim), not the game. Design: DESIGN.md, "Act 4: hell, the mine and the forge (design, not built)". Hell is dark on both pages; the page around it follows the theme. Every frame is at 1x (game scale) or 3x, pixelated.

Placeholders. Nothing in a picture has words. Every player-facing word on the board (section 4) is a placeholder, tinted like this; the toggle below hides the tint, so the sheet can be seen as it would read. Ore, bar, tool and barrier names in captions are working names from DESIGN.md, in italics.

The palette. The deep's ramp, capped at six: black, the deep's ground #26262c, its stone #42424a, lit #6e6e78, pale #b4b4c0, and white. #121216 is gone. Rock is texture and made things are shape. Rock holds to two adjacent dark rungs, the deep's ground and stone, with lit flecks only near fires. Everything built (furnace, anvil, tank, trough, tray, stair, walkway, cage) is drawn a band brighter, with a one-cell black gap round its silhouette and a white bottom row where the floor's fire catches it. Heat with no red: light comes from below (the floor's top row is lit, the air just over it is washed, and a body's bottom rim is white); flames are white, their height and tone driven by smooth value noise over time, as render/flicker.js drives the cauldron's fire; glare is crossfaded between rungs by alpha; embers rise and fade out. There is no row-shifting shimmer, only a few faint motes drifting up through empty air. Purple shows in three places only: the road's portal, God's halo, and God's light going into the souls.

Which ramp hell should use. The scaffold's render/hell.js paints its placeholder floor from the neutral GREY ramp (HELL_FLOOR_TONES in config/hell.js). Proposed: hell draws from the deep's capped ramp through render/deeptones.js (GREYS, and PURPLES for the three magic places), with roles of its own beside DEEP_GREY: ground 4 and stone 6 for rock, lit 8, pale 10, white. The road from the deep then runs into hell as one material, the dark page already gets the deep's handling (landed, with the hollow written raw #000), and hell inherits the six-grey cap and its browser check. This mock draws in those tones as seen.

1. The run of chambers: five camera stops at 1x, and a diagram

Each frame is what the game's camera holds, 70 by 84 cells at 1x: one chamber, with the barrier it is working toward at its right edge. Stop 1, the landing: the portal in the roof and the muster's ladder, the anvil and smith, the quench tank, a furnace, the dross face, and the pair at rubble they cannot yet cut. Through the rubble's gaps the cage room's glow leaks white. Stops 2 to 5: the stair comes in at the upper left from the chamber above, a hauler climbs it with bars for the anvil a step at a time, a miner works the face under it, a furnace with as many white tongues as its heat, and the wall gang taking turns at the barrier from their walkway. Stop 5, the cage room: God in the cage, hung from the roof, the glow filling the room.

The run, as a diagram

The whole run at 2 pixels a cell, a map rather than a picture: rock, the five hollows stepping down and right, the barriers as pale blocks, the portal, and the cage. The dotted boxes are the five camera stops above, left to right.

2. The stations, at 3x

The ore faces: one still motif an ore, readable at one cell

Each motif reads at one cell and holds still: dross mottled blobs, cinder iron horizontal bands, pitch copper pale one-cell diagonal veins on black, obsidian glass big dark facets with a white corner cell, adamant a lattice. A slow sheen crossfades over copper, obsidian and adamant as decoration only. Working: a ghost miner swings in held poses (rest, wind-up held, smear, impact held, follow-through), dust puffs off the face, every third blow a lump drops, and a hauler carries it off. The face is one cell deep where it has been worked. Each ore also appears at 1x beside its 3x.

The furnace: heat as white tongues, and a refused ore

A chamber's heat N shows as N white tongues in the door's bottom row. Cold: door black. Lit, heat 2: two tongues, with the stoker standing by. Stoked, heat 2: the same two tongues, taller and whiter, with the stoker throwing brimstone. Out of fuel: the bin is empty, the tongues are gone, the last embers pulse in the door, and the flue smokes. Refused: a heat-2 furnace with an obsidian lump (tier 4) waiting in the trough beside it. The trough is the furnace's strip, and the pile-full mark is warning() from render/pilemarks.js copied exactly (WARN_R = P * 2.6, bars of r / 4), hung where pileMarkAt would hang it: at the middle of the strip, seven cells under the ground.

Brimstone: the crust round a vent

The vent puffs and licks white (smooth noise). Crust grows round it a crystal at a time, each fading in. A gatherer knocks it off a crystal a blow and carries a lump away.

The anvil and the smith

Idle: the anvil empty, bars and the old pick on the tray. Working: a hauler brings the bars in, the smith works the blank in held strokes, 24 of them (TOOL_STROKES in config/hell.js), and white sparks fade as they fall. The blank cools by crossfade between heats, and the finished pick is carried out.

The quench: black water from the abyss

For the top two tools. A ghost brings a bucket down from the deep and pours it in. The smith dips the white-hot glass-edged pick, steam rises and fades, and the pick comes out dark.

The barriers, each worked by a gang of four in turns

A wall gang of four works in turns from a walkway hung off the roof. The member at the front steps down to the face, swings until a piece comes loose, and carries that piece to the spoil heap along the floor. It drops it there (the heap grows one cell, fading in), climbs the ladder, and walks back along the walkway to the tail of the queue. Meanwhile the next member steps down. Carriers walk only one way on the floor and members only one way on the walkway, so nobody passes anybody. A piece falls as its own cells, lies at the face foot, and is carried overhead as itself. The wall is undercut, two columns at a time from the face, bottom to top. Each barrier reuses its seam's motif cell for cell: rubble is dross blobs with gaps that show the glow; the bone wall is cinder's bands as courses of bone, with knuckle cells; the lava gate is copper's diagonal veins as cracks, with fire showing white through them; once it is breached, fire runs out along the floor's top row and cools by crossfade. The anchor chains are obsidian facets as links, three strands staggered. The cage is adamant's lattice as bars, with God behind it. A 4-second hold and a fade end each loop; the fade is the mock repeating, not a game event. A strip of stills at 1x sits under each.

A new tool arrives, and the pair's own click

A miner at the bone wall with the dross pick: every blow rings off (white rings that open and fade) and nothing comes loose. A carrier walks in with the iron pick, the picks change hands, and the next swing bites and a piece falls. Then the miner steps back, one of the pair steps up, and two clicks (the pointer) land two blows of their own.

3. Assets sheet

Each at 3x. Lumps are 2 by 2 cells and bars 3 by 2, each in its ore's motif, so they read at 1x. Picks are held items, drawn at the body's 2-pixel unit. There are five bars, the plain pitch copper bar among them; the alloy is the tier III pick's recipe (copper and iron bars and the iron pick), not a bar.

The flare: the warning, then the flare

The vent's glow builds by alpha (the serpent's phase-warning register) and the miner steps back. A column of white fire rises to the roof and fades, embers fly, slag crusts the face (fading in), and brimstone crusts round the vent. The miner comes back and clears the crust a blow a cell.

Freeing God, a link at a time

The cage's front is cut away, leaving its frame. God hangs on two chains. The links come off one at a time from God's end, alternating sides, and each falls and lies. God drops, lands and walks out. The halo breathes brighter as each link goes. God's look is a placeholder and the owner's call.

Lives returned

In God's glare a mote of God's light (purple: magic) travels to each soul's rim, and the twelve pieces fade from pale to black as the motes arrive, alternating between the two. Then the middles and the outfits fade to the yard's ink.

4. The forge's board

The shape of the game's sheet, with its tokens copied (light: white paper, black ink; dark: #1f1f1f paper, #e0e0e0 ink, spark #f06a55). Every card is an eight-rung ladder in tierRows bands (HELL_BANDS), and its bill is its next rung's: rungs 1 and 2 brimstone only, 3 and 4 brimstone and low bars, 5 and 6 brimstone and low and high bars, 7 and 8 low and high bars and a spark. Crew rows (smiths, stokers, miners, haulers, gatherers, builders, carriers, muster) are ladders too, not counters: a member put to a face stays there for good. Only the wall gang pulls miners off their faces, so it alone has a stepper. Hell's coins have no color, so only the spark band's pips are red. Flux is the yard's ore carried down, so it keeps the yard's ore mark and blue. Bars: dross dotted, iron solid, copper banded, glass hollow, adamant latticed. Dashed means a coin is short; a gate you cannot try yet is grey and solid. Every word is a placeholder.

What this mock cannot show

  • The game's renderer: the deep's turned palette and the dark page's map, device-pixel snapping, the wardrobe's real outfits. The pair's outfits here are stand-ins.
  • The camera moving: the glide between stops, and walking back up the run with the view's arrows. The frames are five held views.
  • Real pathing: walks are scripted on straight lines and the stair. One hauler climbs each stair, and the gang's walkway keeps the gang's lanes apart. The face miner and a carrier can still overlap where the floor is shared (route.js would queue them).
  • Numbers: blows, burn, batch and pace are made up for the loops; only TOOL_STROKES = 24 is config's.
  • Loop resets: each loop ends in a hold and a fade. In the game a face only deepens and a heap only grows.
  • The pile-full mark is drawn with canvas paths, as the game's warning() is, so its edge is antialiased and not on the grid. That is the game's own mark, copied.
  • Satan (call 4), the muster's arrivals down the whole road, the beats as cutscenes, and sound.
  • A phone, and the real zoom on a real screen.
