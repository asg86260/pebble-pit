Queueing the same row again

A mock drawn from plain boxes, not the game. One made-up model drives every drawing on the page: a purse filling at a steady rate, three rows at one site (a ladder, a count row and a one-off), one builder who walks to the site for each work. Press a tile to buy; press a waiting line on any queue card to hand the newest copy of that row back. Prices are illustrative, not the game's tables. What it cannot show: the real board's layout and fonts, the real walk and the lent body, dust flying grain by grain, the band coins (crops, ore, sparks) on a ladder's bill, several sites sharing one card, or a phone.

The yard and the board

Every press on a row already in the works puts the next one in line, at its own price: the ladder's next rung, the next house. The one-off is refused once it is paid for.

The queue card

The same line, drawn three ways. A waiting line is a button: it hands back that row's newest copy, the dearest, at the price it was paid.

A -- a line a copy

Today's card, with repeats written out. Every copy has its own clock; five rungs of one ladder is five lines.

B -- a line a run, ×n (recommended)

A run of one row is one line. The front's pips are the copy being built; the clock is to the last of the run landing.

C -- a line a run, a box a copy

As B, with the count drawn as boxes: the front one fills as it is built, the rest stand empty.
