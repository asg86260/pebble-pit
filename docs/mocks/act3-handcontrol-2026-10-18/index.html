Act 3 hand plaques (mock)

The camp at its real scale (a world pixel two-thirds of a screen pixel). Working a station by hand happens on a plaque floating over it: a paper card with an ink rule, the one part you move drawn big on it, the speed gauge and the ghost hand on it too. The station below keeps its size and moves with you. The pedestal's bubbles stay where they are, in the sky. Everything is live with a mouse or a finger. Drawn from plain cells by this page, not by the game. Every word on it is a placeholder (marked like this; on the canvas, a star after the word).

Recommendation, pending the owner's vote: (b), click to open. It is the only one of the three that works the same with a mouse, a finger and a pad, it never changes under you while you work, and the camera stays yours.

how it shows page

(a) hover

shows fades in while the pointer is over a station; stays while the pointer is on the plaque or working it; fades out a third of a second after it leaves both

pad a pad has no pointer: the shoulder buttons step a focus along the camp and the focus counts as the hover, so on a pad this is (b)

phone a phone has no hover: a tap opens it, and from there it is (b)

(b) click to open

shows a click on a station opens its plaque; it stays until a click on the sky or floor, or on another station, which swaps them (one fades out as the other fades in)

pad LB / RB step the open plaque along the camp with the camera following; A opens, B closes; the sticks work the part

phone a tap opens it; a tap on the sky or floor closes it; a swipe on the floor pans and the plaque rides along with its station, kept on the glass, and closes once the station is off it

(c) nearest the middle

shows always one plaque, the station nearest the middle of the screen; pan and it hands over to the next; a click on a station slides it to the middle

pad the left stick pans, the right stick works the part: nothing to open or close, the most natural on a pad

phone a swipe pans; at 390 px two or three stations share the glass, so the plaque changes hands every swipe or so; a tap on a station slides it to the middle

on a phone, 390 px wide

The same camp at the same scale: about ninety-seven cells of it on the glass, two or three stations. A swipe on the floor or the sky pans; the plaque is the same size as on the desk, so it is about half the glass wide, and it is kept on the glass at both edges with its tail still pointing at its station.

This frame takes touch the way a phone does: no hover. In (a) a tap opens the plaque, as in (b).

how two plaques keep apart

  • A plaque stands above the tallest thing under it, so it only ever covers sky: never a station, never a neighbor's part, never the pedestal's bubbles' way up (the pedestal is far down the camp).
  • It is wider than a station's spacing (the paper mill's middle and the workshop's are thirty cells, 118 px, apart; a plaque is 180), so it overhangs its neighbors' sky. Only one is open at a time in (b), so that is all it does.
  • While one hands over to another (b swapping, a, or c), the two are on the glass together for a quarter of a second: the leaving one slides aside by the overlap as it fades, the arriving one takes its place over its station. Tick "two at once" to hold both and see the room they make: each moves half the overlap and keeps its tail on its station.
  • The plaque's part is the only thing that takes a press on it: a press on the plaque's paper away from the part does nothing, so a slip never closes it or works a neighbor.

What the mock cannot show

  • The real renderer: these are the built stations' cells ported into this page, not after.js, its layers, its crowd or its members walking in.
  • The real camera: the scale is fixed at two-thirds here, with no zoom toward God and no lift.
  • The boards: the camp's cards (the shack's, the stations') are not drawn, so how a plaque sits beside an open board is not shown.
  • Gamepad feel: the pad lines are text only. Nothing here reads a pad.
  • A real phone: the frame is a 390 px canvas on this page. Thumb reach, the browser's own swipe-back, and a finger hiding the part are not shown.
  • Pacing: every speed and payout is the gestures mock's guess, and nothing is spent or carried.
