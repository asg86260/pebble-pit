Act 3 card tabs (mock)

The card a click opens over an act 3 station gets tabs: work (the hand-work face, as built) and upgrades (the station's posts and named upgrades). The construction shack's card has one tab, build; the pedestal's has one, upgrades, because its work is popping bubbles in the sky. The camp is at its real scale. The rows are the yard board's tiles, styled from what the game's stylesheet computes on the card bench. Bills are config/after.js's. Click a station to open its card, a tab to switch, a tile to buy from the fake purse. Drawn by this page, not by the game. Every word on it is a placeholder (marked like this; on the game's own surfaces, a star after the word).

Recommendation, pending the owner's vote: (C), the card keeps its height. The card never grows up into the sky, so its tail and leader stay put and the station under it stays in view. The posts sit on the work tab, beside the part those members work. Upgrades is always one plank, two or three tiles, and it scrolls sideways on a phone. The tiles are the yard's own.

What the mock cannot show

  • The real board builder: these tiles are hand-built HTML with the game's computed styles copied in, not mountRows. The glyphs are placeholder drawings (act 3's rows have none yet).
  • The house face: Courier New stands in for Courier Prime, so widths are a few pixels off.
  • The real renderer and camera: the stations are ported cells, the scale is fixed, and the built card is drawn on the canvas, not in the page as it is here.
  • Nothing teleports in the game: there, a bill's yard coins fly up through the cloud floor to the station, a member walks from the camp to a new post, and a bought station is raised a piece at a time. Here a buy just changes the numbers, and a station fades in.
  • Gamepad feel: the pad table is text only. Nothing here reads a pad.
  • A real phone: the frame is a 390 px box on this page. Thumb reach and the browser's own gestures are not shown.
  • Wording: the act's coins are said as words in a tag (rebel, timber, paper), which makes tags wider than the yard's. A mark for them is the owner's call.
