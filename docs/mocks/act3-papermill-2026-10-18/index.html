The paper mill: the vat and line, making paper you can watch

A playable mock drawn from plain cells on the game's grid (P = 6, the game's eleven greys, a body the game's 18-pixel square with a 2-pixel rim), not the game. It is take A, vat and line, from src/render/after/stations.js with the same vat, step, paddle and line, grown a mould, a pulley cord, a longer line, a stack and a peel. The big picture is the 1x drawing scaled three times; the 1x row further down is the same yard at the game's zoom.

Every word on this page is a placeholder (marked like this, and the whole page is): station, job, tool and upgrade names are working names. The canvases draw no words, only the stack's count. The owner writes the player's words.

Stir: press on the vat in the big picture and drag round in circles (mouse or a finger). Faster circles fill the batch faster, up to a cap. When a batch is full the pulper hauls the mould out on its cord and pegs the sheet; the loft hand takes dry sheets off the far end with the peel.

The drying fade, held, 3x

Five sheets stopped at 0, 25, 50, 75 and 100 per cent dry, then a wet sheet's lean and a dry sheet's lean to either side (the difference on the line is how often each leans, not how far). On the line the fade is continuous (each sheet's two tones are blended by how dry it is, never stepped); here it is stopped so the tones can be compared.

What each step looks like

  1. Stir. The paddle is plumb and steps a whole cell at a time across the vat, following the hand's circle; a crest of pulp stands ahead of it and a trough opens behind. The batch filling shows in the pulp: thin pale grey after a sheet is pulled, thickening cell by cell (each cell fades on its own) to a darker mottled grey when the batch is full. The pulper on the step leans with the paddle.
  2. The mould lifts out. A cord runs from the pulper's hand up the left post, along the line, and down to a hook over the vat; the mould hangs on it under the pulp. With the batch full the pulper leans back and hauls, hand over hand (the body rocks two pixels a step), and the mould rises a cell a step out of the pulp: two black side bars, a top and bottom rail, and the wet sheet in it in the darkest sheet grey. Drops fall from its bottom rail back into the vat and vanish at the surface.
  3. Pegged, still wet. At the line two pegs fade in at the sheet's top corners; the mould sinks back behind the sheet, empty, into the pulp. The new sheet hangs dark grey, its bottom row a step darker where the water pools, and drips fall under it into the vat.
  4. It dries. Over sixteen seconds the sheet fades from that grey to paper: the body first, the pooled bottom row last. Drips thin out as it dries and stop. In dark mode the same fade runs from mid grey to the paper tone #1f1f1f inside an ink outline.
  5. It sways. A gust runs down the line on a held clock (a pose every 0.3 s), so the sheets lean in a ripple from the vat end outward. A lean is the sheet's bottom row kicked a whole cell to one side, the rim following it. A wet sheet is heavy: it kicks only at a gust's peak, so it mostly hangs plumb. A dry sheet kicks at most gusts and swings through to the other side.
  6. Off the line, onto the stack. When the far end's sheet is dry, the loft hand raises the peel (a plumb pole with a level head) a cell a step until the head is under the sheet; the pegs fade off; the sheet rides the head down to the stack and fades out as the new top sheet fades in and the count ticks. As soon as the far end is free the line slides one slot along, a cell a step, so the slot over the vat is open before the next batch is full. If the far end is still wet the line cannot slide, and a full batch waits in the vat with the pulper's hands on the cord.
  7. Double output. The bigger vat lifts a two-sheet mould: one haul, two sheets, two slides. On the short line that outruns the drying (the far end is still wet when the next pair needs room, so batches wait); the drying line doubles the line and the stack walks out with it, so every sheet gets the same sixteen seconds and the pair keeps up with the fastest hand.

Harsh critique, round 1 (and what was changed)

  1. The cord read as a second post. A two-pixel line beside the left post, ending in nothing at the top. Changed: a pulley block (two cells, a white eye) on the post's top, so the cord visibly runs over something.
  2. The count sat on the vat's wall. It was drawn left of the stack and landed on the right wall in the 1x row. Changed: it sits over the stack, above the peel's head.
  3. The resting peel was a table. A level head held at four cells over the stack read as a shelf with the count hung under it. Changed: at rest the peel's head lies on the stack, and it lands there with each sheet.
  4. The handle went through the rising mould. When the hand kept stirring while the pulper hauled, the level handle still ran to the pulper's hand across the mould: one body doing two jobs. Changed: while the pulper hauls, the hand holds the paddle by a short crossbar; the handle only reaches the body when the body is at the paddle.
  5. Two-row leans read as torn sheets. A dry sheet with its middle and bottom rows both kicked a cell was a stair with a notch, at 3x and at 1x. Changed: a lean is the bottom row only; dry sheets lean at most gusts and to both sides, wet ones only at a gust's peak. "More freely" is now how often, not how far.
  6. The doubled output did not double. Measured, the two-sheet mould on the long line made 15.8 sheets a minute at the hand's cap, not 24: the loft hand and the line's two slides were run one after the other while the full batch waited. Changed: the line slides as soon as the far end is free (while the batch is still filling), and every step of the haul and the peel was quickened; now 21.8.

Harsh critique, round 2 (and what was changed)

  1. Dark mode reads backwards to the eye. Mapped by lightness, the wet sheet is a light grey and a dry one goes to the paper tone, #1f1f1f, so in dark the sheets darken as they dry, which is how wet cloth looks, not dry. The progression reads, and the pooled bottom row and the drips say which end is wet, but it is the first thing to vote on. Not changed: the mapping is the owner's rule; breaking it for one station is a call for the owner.
  2. A full batch waiting showed nothing. The hand could circle a full vat with no sign of why nothing happened. Changed: while a batch waits on the line the pulper stands leaning back with hands on the cord. Still weak at 1x (a two-pixel lean); the honest cue is the wet sheet at the far end.
  3. The long line is mostly air. At 1x the doubled station is 60 cells, its right half an empty frame on a white backing, and it dwarfs the yard tower beside it. The game's paper mill has 36 cells of ground. Not changed: this is the price of the long line; the alternative (two lines stacked) does not work mechanically, see the open questions.
  4. The pulper alone shows only two sheets on the line. At eight seconds a batch and sixteen to dry, two wet sheets hang at a time and dry sheets wait at the far end; the full four-stage gradient only shows at a fast hand. Not changed: it is the honest count. A longer drying time would show more stages, but then the short line stalls the fastest hand.
  5. The empty twin mould sinking looks like a lid on the vat. For the half second its rails cross the pulp surface it is a flat box over the vat. Not changed: it passes in a few frames; a thinner rail would break the cell-thick rule.
  6. The stack is still the game's ruled stack. At 3x it reads as a slatted crate rather than paper. Not changed: it is sheetPile as built, kept so the camp's piles agree; flagged in case the owner wants paper to read as paper here.

What the mock cannot show

  • The real camp: the cloud floor, the posts along each station's top, the build in bands, the glyph stack over a working station, the pile-full mark, and whether a 40-cell (or, with the long line, 60-cell) station fits the camp's spacing. The game's ART box for the paper mill is 36 cells of ground.
  • Where the pulp comes from. Chips arrive from the chipper by carrier in the game; here the vat never runs dry.
  • Who carries paper on to the press: carriers taking sheets off the stack are not drawn.
  • The hand with no pulper posted. Here a body always stands on the step and does the haul; who lifts the mould when only the player is stirring is an open question (below).
  • The game's gesture numbers are copied (six turns a batch, 1.2 turns a second at most, the ease and coast), but the hand's box and the vat ellipse are this page's, not after/place.js's.

Open questions for the owner

  • The loft hand is a second body at the station. It could instead be the pulper climbing down between batches (slower, one job), or a carrier from the workshop's pool. Which?
  • With the hand stirring and nobody posted, should the mould lift by itself (the hand's batch), or wait for a body?
  • The doubled line makes the station 60 cells wide. The other way to double it, two lines one above the other, was tried and drops out: the peel cannot reach the upper line's end through the lower line, and the second mould would have to rise through the lower line's sheets.
