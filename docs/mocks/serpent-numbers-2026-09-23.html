Reading the fight: four fonts for the serpent's numbers

The owner's call: numbers, no splats, and not in the world's 6 px cells -- a font of their own. A mock drawn from plain cells, not the game: the same scripted twenty-two seconds of fight as the serpent-hits mock (a brawler punches 6 a blow, a lance bleeds, a grenade's burst, a wizard's beam and curse, a sigil, a star for 60, then the heal), with option A's behavior in every pane -- a number rises from where a blow lands and fades out through the tones; the lance and the beam say their total once a second; a grenade's whole burst is one number; the heal rises off the wound in purple as +N once a second. What differs is only the font. The top row is the game's real scale: a world cell is 6 screen pixels, as at the yard's own zoom on a 1920x1080 screen, and each pane is a 72x34-cell stretch of that screen. The second row is the camera zoomed in 2x: the world doubles and every number stays the size it was, because all four are drawn in screen pixels, as the counter card is. What it cannot show: the real abyss and camera, the real bodies and weapons (squares and lines here), a crowd of numbers from many weapons at once, the stages' tones, and the numbers are the mock's, not the game's.
