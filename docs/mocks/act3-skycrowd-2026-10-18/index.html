Act 3: God's crowd fills the sky

This is a mock drawn from plain shapes in a canvas. It is not the game: there is no sim and no game renderer. It uses the game's grid (P = 6, a body is 3 cells), its eleven greys and the dark page's lightness map, at the game's zoom 1. Every name on this page in dotted underline is a placeholder word, and so are God, the throne, the signs, the posters and every symbol in a bubble.

This draws the owner's decisions as they were given. The crowd fills the whole sky and ours behind the camp is dropped. The crowd's life is rolling waves plus rallies. God sits high in the center on a tower throne, with a halo, rays and a searchlight gaze. There is a symbol sermon and a burning speech with a gaze warning, and a tithe line climbing to the glare. Small souls drift up. Panning right zooms in on God. The mock decides nothing beyond those.

2000 by 967, zoom 1

How each piece reads

  • The crowd fills the sky. There are seven parallax bands, from the cloud floor up to near the top of the sky. The five far bands are cached grey cells: 2, 2, 3, 4 and 5 px heads. The top band is palest (grey 1.6) and each band below it is a step darker. Each band's top two rows of heads are a step darker again, so every band's lumpy edge reads against the one behind it. Band 6 is staggered 6 px heads. The nearest band, just above the cloud floor, is 12 px souls, each with an ink rim, two eyes looking toward God, and its own paper tone. The bands pan at 0.24 to 0.80 of the camp's rate, and every cell takes a tone a step either side of its band's grey.
  • Thin clouds drift between bands 2 and 3, 4 and 5, and 5 and 6. Each is a broken billow of near-white cells, one to three cells tall, moving slowly on its own clock.
  • Thinning. Band k (0 at the back) starts to thin at 12% + 11% × k of support, so the back band goes first, and each band is gone 26% after it starts. Its columns drop in uneven holes, its top sinks, and at the end it fades out. At 100% only a scatter of the near band is left. Near souls fade one at a time, so no band steps out.
  • Joining. About once a second and a half, a near soul's rim goes black, holds half a second, and the soul fades out. The members count goes up as it inks. (In the mock it fades back in, pale, fourteen seconds later. See "cannot show".)
  • A, the sea. A long wave rolls through every band at once. Heads rise and fall a cell at a time, as held steps rather than a glide, and the near souls also sway a cell across.
  • C, a rally. Every six to nine seconds a sign or poster lifts a cell. A ring of raised arms (two ink strokes over each soul) spreads out from it through the near band, and as a lifted ridge of heads through the far bands.
  • God sits high in the center, in front of the five far bands and behind the two near ones. He is a near-black square with pale almond eyes slanted into a scowl, under a pale crown in an ink outline. Below the square is a mid-grey robe with a pale center seam, and his hands rest on the throne's arms. The throne's back is a mid-grey slab with five ink spikes, so the dark square stands off it. It sits on a tall, narrow, slit-windowed tower that rises out of the crowd to the cloud floor. His idle loop is in held poses. He breathes: the square's top rises a cell, holds and falls. His pupils follow the gaze, and the top of each eye shuts to a squint while it lingers on the camp. The right hand's three fingers drum one at a time, then rest. Every eleven seconds the crown slips a step on one side. Three seconds later the left hand comes up in two poses, levels it and goes down.
  • The halo is a grey ring behind his head, with a fainter inner ring and short radial marks between the two, so it reads faintly as an iris. Twelve spokes, long and short in turn, orbit it slowly. They shorten and fade as support rises and are gone at 100%.
  • The gaze. Two dotted grey edges run from his eyes, and below the seat a pale wedge of light lies across the crowd. It sweeps right, center and left, then down onto the camp, where it holds for two and a half seconds. The camp's bodies look up while it is there.
  • The sermon. About every six and a half seconds, God roars a symbol bubble ("!!", "?!", "#!", a skull and "!", or a bolt) and his mouth opens. A ring rolls out from the throne through every band, and heads duck a cell as it passes; the near souls also shut their eyes. Between roars, small grey grumble bubbles ("~", "...") send a short ring that lifts the far heads and harms nothing.
  • The burning speech runs on "speech now", and on its own every thirty seconds. It picks a sign both windows can see. The gaze swings off its sweep onto the sign and settles there, narrowing. Four ink corner marks close in on the sign in three held steps: that is the warning, and it lasts about two seconds. Then God roars a bolt and the ring rolls out. The sign chars to ink cell by cell from the bottom, burns away cell by cell with embers rising, and is gone. (In the mock it fades back thirteen seconds later.)
  • The tithe line is seventy bowed carriers, head low in front, each with a plank or a sheet on its back. It leaves the camp, crosses the cloud floor and zigzags up through the crowd on a dotted ledge to a pale glare left of the throne. The carriers shrink with depth, and their parallax eases from the camp's rate to God's. The glare takes the load first and then the carrier. As support rises the line thins and slows and its ledge fades. At 100% what is left stands still.
  • Small souls drift up from the camp every few seconds, fading in and out. There are no spires and no dashed horizon line; the cloud floor's lumpy billow is the only horizon.
  • The camera. Every layer pans at its own rate. Panning right brings in a zoom about God's face, which rises to 2.6x at the right end and pulls his face to the middle of the frame. Edges are rounded after the zoom, so strokes stay whole.

Harsh self-critique, round 1 (the first build's shots)

  • Readable? No: the clouds read as a dashed horizon line, which the owner ruled out. They were thin white dashes in rows, three of them across the sky. Fix: broken billows one to three cells tall, with lumpy tops.
  • Readable? No: the sky was one grey felt. The band edges did not show, so it read as static rather than as layers. Fix: a pale-to-dark ramp from the top band down, a darker top row of heads on every band, and less speckle (a step either side on 1 cell in 6, not 1 in 3).
  • The back band was already gone at 10%. Thinning started at 0. Fix: the back band starts at 12% of support, so 10% is a full sky.
  • God was a black blob on a black throne, with eyes that read as four white dots. Fix: the throne's back is now mid grey, so the square stands off it. The eyes are slanted almonds, one pupil each, and they scowl. The robe is mid grey with a seam, so the square still reads as a square.
  • God was off the top of the frame, with the spikes cut off. Fix: he sits lower.
  • The halo was hidden behind the throne, because its ring was smaller than the throne's back. Fix: a ring of 20 God-cells.
  • Exciting? The burning sign was invisible. A 42 px sign charring on a 2000-wide frame went unseen, and so did the warning frame (2 px of grey). Fix: signs at 60 by 36 and posters at 42 by 60. The warning is now four ink corner marks that close in in held steps, the way a target is marked without a flash.
  • The gaze painted a stripe down God's own robe. Fix: the light pool starts below the seat, and only its dotted edges run up to the eyes.

Harsh self-critique, round 2 (after the fixes)

  • Exciting? The roar, the big beat, is a one-cell duck. In the near band it reads: the souls drop and shut their eyes. In the far bands it is a faint ridge on a 2000-wide frame. The rally is the same: two clear arcs of raised arms in the near band, and little above it. Both rings may want a tone step on the far bands as they pass, flat and held. That is not drawn here.
  • Readable at 800 by 600? Busy in the middle. God, the halo, the gaze, the tithe zigzag and two signs share about 460 px of sky, and the zigzag crosses the tower. At 2000 by 967 it has room. The small window may want two zigzags, not three.
  • The holes read as pillars. A band thins by whole columns, so at 50% the far bands show vertical stripes and gaps. That reads more as a broken wall than as a thinning crowd. Holes by patch rather than by column would look more like people leaving.
  • Thinning reads, but not as walking out. 90% plainly looks emptier than 10%, but nothing goes toward the camp: a soul fades where it stands. That is what was decided ("fades out and counts as a member"), but the camp never visibly gains, and the counter is the only reward.
  • Dark mode inverts the mood. Under the lightness map God's near-black becomes near-white, so in dark mode he is a pale square. The searchlight's paper becomes the dark grey, so it is a cone of shadow, and the glare is a dark disc. The dotted edges keep the gaze readable, but "Sauron" reads only in light mode. If he must be dark in both modes, he needs an exception to the map.
  • On an empty sky the gaze vanishes. At 90% the pale wedge lies on paper and only the dots show. That may be right, since nothing is left to light, but it means the gaze fades out with the crowd.
  • God at zoom 1 is small for "giant". The square is 108 px in the 2000-wide window and 54 px in 800 by 600, three bodies across in the small one. The tower and halo give him size, but the face reads as menacing only at the zoomed right end or in the 3x crop.
  • Off the P = 6 grid. These are all finer than P = 6: the far bands' cells (2 to 5 px), the halo, rays and iris marks (2 or 4 px), the spike on God's center line, and God's own cells in the small window (3 px). It is deliberate, for distance, but it breaks the house grid, and the owner should accept or refuse it.

What the mock cannot show

  • The frame cost in the real renderer. The number under each canvas is this page's own draw time. In a headless shell it was about 8 to 11 ms a frame for the 2000-wide canvas and 1 to 2 ms for 800 by 600 (most of it the far bands' slices and the near souls). The five far bands are cached bitmaps, drawn as column slices, and the near souls, the cloud floor and the carriers are a few thousand rects. The game draws through its own layers, ink map and camera, beside the camp and its sim. Nobody has measured this in the game. The full-sky crowd is several times the area of the current mass, so expect it to cost more than today's three bands.
  • The sim. The thinning here is a noise field shaped by the slider, not a count of souls. A join is a random near soul on a timer, and it fades back after fourteen seconds so the mock does not run out. A burnt sign fades back too. In the game, neither would return.
  • How the camp and the stations look today. The camp here is a few placeholder boxes and four bodies.
  • What the player does when zoomed in on God, and how the real camera pans: by drag, wheel or key, and how it comes back.
  • Sound, weather, smog, and the act's other cutscenes.
  • Whether the symbol bubbles are readable as anger without any words, to a new player.
  • Every sign, poster, glyph and the God figure itself are placeholders, each still waiting on its own mock.
