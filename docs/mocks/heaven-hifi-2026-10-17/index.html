Heaven, high fidelity (act 3: the grove and the press)

A mock, drawn from plain shapes in a canvas, not the game: nothing here runs the sim or the game's renderer. It is drawn on the game's own grid (P = 6, a body three cells across with a two-pixel rim) from the game's own ramps (palette.js: the eleven greys and the purple). Game scale is one world pixel to one screen pixel, the way the yard is drawn; the close-ups are the same drawing scaled three times. Design: DESIGN.md, "Acts 3 and 4: the afterlife and hell (design, not built)", the loop "the grove and the press".

Every word a player would read on this page is a placeholder. The owner writes the copy. The canvases carry no words at all; the board in section 4 is all placeholder text, dotted underlined.

1. The establishing view: heaven late in act 3

One wide place, left to right as the design has it: the grove (old growth and planted plots, a feller at work, a planter at a stump, haulers taking logs to the mill), the camp (saw mill and its sawdust heap at the brim, paper mill, workshop, printing press, the ink well under a storm cloud, the faith plate under a puffed cloud, the hall), the thin patch (the yard below shows through; risen souls come up here, and the ghost road lands here at the end), God's yard (the great cloud shedding mist onto heaps, the crowd of the dead working it, a fence papered with posters, signs planted in the crowd with ink rings round them, a warden tearing a poster), and the stair up to the throne in its glare, the tithe line climbing it and a warden carrying a sign up into the glare. A thunderhead drifts across with its shadow on the floor.

2. Each station at 3x, idle and working, with its workers

Stations here are high key, as the design asks: built of pale timber with black rims, not the yard's solid black mass. Every worker is a member (a body with its rim fully inked). Poses are held, never tweened: a swing has a raised frame, a held impact and a recovery.

3. The new assets

Every new drawing on its own, at 3x. Animated ones loop.

The unmasking, as a strip

The strike has starved the glare. It comes off by opacity, a cell at a time, never a pop, outside in; under it is the same shape drawn the deep's way, inverted. God's own look (here a big square with a three-point crown) and Satan's two purple eyes are placeholders for the mock-vote the design calls for.

What this mock cannot show

  • The game's renderer: device-pixel snapping, the wardrobe's real outfits on the pair (a plain top hat and bow stand in), the LAYERS painting order, the real pile-full mark and tooltip, the real cursor sprite.
  • Any numbers, pacing or balance: how fast a tree falls, how many chops, how fast ink spreads. The loops here are timed to read, not tuned.
  • A crowd of thousands. Four ranks of a few dozen souls are drawn; the far files thinning into cloud cells is described, not drawn. Support spreading is shown as a fixed map, not the sim (signs, posters, word of mouth).
  • The camera and the beats (the house falling, the arrival, the meeting, the axe handed over, the strike's cutscene, Satan's line, the descent), the throne's speech sweep and its warning, and the dark-mode treatment of a new place: dark here simply flips the grey ramp, keeping the glare white and purple purple.
  • The real board: the game's tile builder, its subgrid, glyph canvases and hover plate. The card in section 4 is plain CSS in the menu's shape; its glyphs are first drafts, not on the glyph editor yet.
  • God's and Satan's looks, the thin patch's purple lip and Satan's eyes are placeholders for a later vote; the purple is there only because both are magic.
  • Any word a player would read: none are written; every label is the owner's to write.
