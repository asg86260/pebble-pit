Heaven, high fidelity (act 3: the grove and the press)

A mock, drawn from plain shapes in a canvas, not the game: nothing here runs the sim or the game's renderer. It is drawn on the game's own grid (P = 6, a body three cells across with a two-pixel rim; every station, crowd column, sign and trunk placed on a whole cell; no stroke thinner than two pixels) from the game's own ramps (palette.js: the eleven greys and the purple). Game scale is one world pixel to one screen pixel, the way the yard is drawn; the close-ups are the same drawing scaled three times. Design: DESIGN.md, "Acts 3 and 4: the afterlife and hell (design, not built)", the loop "the grove and the press".

Every word a player would read on this page is a placeholder. The owner writes the copy. The canvases carry no words at all; the board in section 4 is all placeholder text, dotted underlined.

Round 2 (review round 1 applied): every loop ends where it starts; nothing pops, every fade is continuous; every link of the chain is carried by a body; wardens, souls, members and the pair are told apart by shape, not by four greys; the dark page is the game's (#e0e0e0 on #1f1f1f, mapped by lightness), with clouds drawn as shade so a storm cloud stays darker than a white one on either page.

1. The establishing view: heaven late in act 3

One wide place, left to right as the design has it. The grove: old growth outside the rope, the planted plots inside it; a feller fells an old tree, two haulers carry its logs to the saw mill's log pile and set them down, a planter grubs the stump and sets a sapling, and the tree grows back (growth sped up so the loop closes). The camp, every link carried by a body: sawdust in sacks from the heap to the paper mill, timber from the mill to the workshop, paper from the paper mill to the press, ink from the well to the workshop and the press. A pledge keeper (a member with an inked middle) stands at the saw mill. The faith plate has a gatherer beating a white cloud; the pointer hits the thunderhead for its burst. The hall takes in a member who was canvassed out of a soul that rose through the thin patch. God's yard: the great cloud shedding mist onto a heap, four ranks of the dead working it, posters on the fence, signs inking rings round them (the askew one inks nothing), word of mouth filling the gaps between members. A carrier plants a sign at the front of the crowd; a warden walks down from the stair, pulls it up and carries it up into the glare. A paster pastes a poster on the cloud pillar; the pillar's warden tears it down. The stair: the tithe line climbs and fades into the glare at the top; new carriers fade in at the foot.

2. Each station at 3x, idle and working, with its workers

Stations are high key, built of pale timber with black rims, and each has one solid ink mass so it reads at 1x: the saw mill's blade housing, the workshop's tool wall, the paper mill's vat, the press's head and platen. Every worker is a member. Poses are held, never tweened. Each working panel shows the station's inputs arriving and its outputs leaving, carried by couriers who walk in and out of frame.

Every new drawing on its own, at 3x. Animated ones loop, and every loop ends where it began.

The unmasking, as a strip

The strike has starved the glare. It comes off by opacity, a cell at a time, never a pop, outside in; under it is the same shape drawn the deep's way, inverted, on a dark field on either page. God's own look (a big square with a three-point crown) and Satan's two purple eyes are placeholders for the mock-vote the design calls for.

4. A heaven board: the saw mill's card

The game's sheet and plank tiles, drawn in plain CSS (not the game's builder or stylesheet). One card of rows, every ladder eight rungs in four bands of two, the bill deepening band by band in act 3's order. Pips stand down the right edge, foot first. Support is drawn on the crowd, never counted, so the purse carries no support figure. Rows marked "proposal" are not in the design. Every word on the sheet and in the purse is a placeholder.

What this mock cannot show

  • The game's renderer: device-pixel snapping, the wardrobe's real outfits on the pair (a plain top hat and bow stand in), the LAYERS painting order, the real cursor sprite. The warning mark copies warning()'s shape, not its code path or tooltip.
  • Any numbers, pacing or balance: how many chops a tree takes, how fast ink spreads, how quickly a tree grows back (sped up here only so each loop closes).
  • A crowd of thousands: four ranks of a few dozen souls are drawn; the far files thinning into cloud cells is described, not drawn. Support is a fixed map plus the canvassing and the ring tile, not the sim.
  • The camera and the story beats (the house falling, the arrival, the meeting, the strike's cutscene, Satan's line, the descent); the throne's speech sweep appears only in the askew-sign tile.
  • Stock that changes over a loop: piles, stacks and heaps are held level by couriers taking out what the station puts in, so the loops can close; the real game's piles rise and fall.
  • The real board: the game's tile builder, its subgrid, glyph canvases and hover plate. Section 4's glyphs are first drafts, not on the glyph editor.
  • God's and Satan's looks, the thin patch's purple lip, Satan's eyes, the keeper's inked middle and the pair's own grey are placeholders for later votes.
  • Any word a player would read: none are written; every label is the owner's to write.
