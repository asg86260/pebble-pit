# Nine critics play the game — 2026-09-10

Nine agents, each a persona, each in its own worktree at `c87c485`, each with
its own dev server and the node yard. They were told to be hard, to bring
evidence, and to end every finding with one concrete change. Their nine reports
are appended in full below; this front section is the triage — every finding
deduplicated, ranked, and cross-referenced to the personas that found it
independently.

The personas: **newcomer** (five minutes, no manual), **idle veteran** (pacing
by the numbers), **UI/UX critic**, **art director**, **systems economist**,
**works watcher** (nothing teleports), **late-game player** (hour six and on),
**returning player** (reloads, saves, tabs), **copy editor**.

Shots and probe scripts live in each critic's worktree under
`.claude/worktrees/agent-*/shots/` and `tmp-critic/`; each report names its
own path. Nothing was committed by any critic.

## Takeaways

**The game has not been played end-to-end by anyone since the shack landed.**
Four critics independently found that shack rungs stop landing after one per
rock hand. It kills the ram. Every balance table in DESIGN.md, TODO.md and the
grind pass was derived from a bot that could not reach a machine — and nobody
noticed, because the bodies keep mining while stuck, so the yard *looks* fine.
A blocker with no visible symptom survived several waves. The gap is
structural: no check runs the whole arc through the shop; "buy it like a
player" has been applied per feature, never across features.

**Hauling is the real economy, and the shop sells everything except it.** The
economist's marginal table and the veteran's flat 274/min curve say the same
thing: the constraint is bodies carrying, and one line in `hauler.js`
(`busy < 1`) throttles all three ground coins to zero. The boards then sell
"+16%" on stations stood down behind a full heap, and nothing on any sheet
says why. Two rules follow: measure every ladder against the walk, not the
number it multiplies (the quarry speed ladder multiplies a floor); and let a
board say the thing the yard already draws (the triangle).

**The rules that make the game distinctive are the ones most often quietly
broken.** "Nothing teleports" — a hat materializes 1,000 px from the school,
the tower credits a wizard in transit, quarriers freeze through the dance.
"Black and white only" — at CELL=5 every sprite edge is grey; the endgame frame
is 10.7% grey against 2.8% black. "The sky stays a live decision" — a maxed
house makes it permanently clean. None of these were designed away; each
slipped in through a mechanism that did not know the rule (`ARRIVED.wizards`,
the device-pixel fit, `MACHINE_MAX_BEATS`). The rules need asserting: a check
that fails when a counter moves with nobody through the door, a pixel-sampling
check that fails on grey, a sky check that runs three tuned machines against a
maxed house and demands rain.

**The first two minutes lose the player before any of the depth matters.** The
newcomer quits between 40 seconds and 2 minutes, from four small things
stacked: the intro hands over the body on the rock (against DESIGN's own text),
the camera stays over the pit so the bench call is invisible, the only
unsticking control (`—`) has no tooltip, and the cheapest first row upgrades
drag capacity — a mechanic never shown. Each is an S fix. The opening has been
the most carefully designed part and the least verified, because every
look.mjs scene starts from `__reset()`, which skips the intro.

**The late game has content the player cannot reach, and the reachable part has
no decision.** Ten hours: 243k spores, 62k shards, 27 cores, tuning rungs past
4 measurably identical, the torn pit empty for 3 h 44 m, buys after hour 6 =
`house ×4`. The red rungs in "The ladder" are the designed sink and are not
built. Fix the sim first — but the veteran's point stands regardless: nothing
compounds. Bodies are the only real income rung and the house curve outruns
the shop at ~22 bodies.

**The instruments were lying, and every critic had to fix them before
measuring.** `RICH` tears the rift, so half the look scenes shoot the wrong
thing; `endgame` buys rows that no longer exist; every RICH shot carries a
BUILD THE BENCH button because `__fullSites` never raises the bench; look.mjs
shoots mid-fade; the greedy playbot spends every 84-dust rung on sight, so the
grind table measures the bot's policy, not the prices. Two critics
independently wrote a `saver` bot. Land D1–D3 first; a wrong instrument costs
more than a wrong number.

**Saves are safe against the game and unsafe against the browser.** Reloads
are genuinely good — worker-seconds, hands, the old-schema fixture all intact.
The two run-losers are outside the sim: a second tab clobbers the save every
second, and an unparseable blob is discarded and replaced with the intro within
a second. Both silent, both irreversible, both S fixes.

**The copy is one register short of done.** Names are consistent; notes are
missing on 80 of 105 rows and run in four registers where they exist. The
uncommitted `Digging upgrades` edit on `rows-shack.js` is the tension in one
line — information against flavor. The copy editor's answer: effect in the
yard's words, no period, no joke; flavor lives in the name. The tower spells
already do this; make it the rule.

**On the process.** Persona critics with disjoint briefs converge on the real
bugs, and the convergence count is a better severity signal than any one
critic's rating: A1 was a BLOCKER for all four who hit it; CELL=5 was one
critic's BLOCKER and nobody else's. Every critic's best evidence came from a
script it wrote in `tmp-critic/` — the shack-stall repro, the sky matrix, the
two-tab driver, the marginal table — and several are worth promoting to
`tools/node/` or to tests before those worktrees are cleaned. The first launch
died on a rate limit with eight of nine mid-run and nothing was recoverable;
critics should checkpoint (the late-game bot did, and that is the only reason a
10-hour run exists).

## The shape of it

Four critics who never spoke to each other found the **same simulation bug**
from four directions: a shack rung bought after one-per-rock-hand never lands
(A1). It kills the ram, and with it every machine number in every balance
table. Three found the **single find-fetcher** rule (A3) that starves shards
and sparks, and two found the **intro handing over a stuck yard** (A2). These
are not balance questions; they are defects that make every balance
measurement below provisional. Fix A1–A5, re-run the bot matrix, *then* tune.

After the sim is honest, the second layer is the one the veteran and the
economist agree on: **income does not compound** — a one-body yard clicking at
4/s earns 274 dust/min flat for six hours and the fully-bought 17-body yard
earns ~400. Everything red is behind 40 shards nobody can mint. And once the
red economy is reachable, the late-game player has already shown that the
**sky goes permanently clean** with a maxed house (a decided rule, violated),
that **tuning rungs past 4 are indistinguishable**, and that the **torn pit
stands empty for hours**.

The third layer is the presentation: one grey (#cfcfcf) that hides exactly
the words a player needs, a BUILD THE BENCH button that clamps over boards and
cutscenes, sprites antialiased into grey at the default cell size, 80 of 105
rows with no note, and a first control (`—` under the rock) that nothing on
screen explains.

Frame cost is fine (median 0.48 ms at tune 12); nobody found a perf problem.

## What the critics said to keep

- The 20-second opening: two squares, a heart, a rock — understood without a word. The single best frame in the game; add nothing to it. (newcomer, art)
- Every currency enters the purse through one door (`bankDust` / `throughRift`) — a grain tipped by a body; the quarry and farm mint nothing until a body is at the face; machines stop within a frame of the tender stepping away; hires walk out of the house. (works watcher)
- Reloads: worker-seconds, hands, places and the old-schema real save all come back intact. (returning)
- The five coin words never slip; the rung grammar is one grammar across nine boards; the tower spell notes are the register everything else should copy. (copy)
- Pinned board width, purse beside the price, the pile-full triangle with a plain-words tip, the dial popup. (UI)
- The tear's dust looping into the disc, the jaw's plume as discrete specks, the abyss with no flat fill. (art)
- `billOf` and `tierRows`: a repricing is one constant. The casino is honestly even-money. The bot is deterministic to the minute. (economist, veteran)

## What I did

## Verdict

The economy has one real constraint and the boards never mention it: **hauler throughput**. Every ground ladder, every plot and bench, every hat, the whole spark supply and therefore every machine is throttled by how many bodies carry and by a one-line rule in `hauler.js` that lets exactly one hauler fetch finds while the rock's heap is backed up. On the greedy 6-hour run the farm heap is full from minute 45 to the end, the quarry heap is full from hour 4, the sky heap is full three minutes after the first wizard, and the result is 43,000 spores and 22 cores hoarded against **0 sparks, 35 shards, no machine, no rift, no scrubbing house at six hours**. Underneath that, the quarry's twelve-rung speed ladder does nothing at any depth, and the crit ladders and the bracing tonic do nothing at the quarry either. This persona quits around hour three: the second and third coins have visibly stopped moving, the four boards are selling "+16%" on stations that are stood down, and nothing on any sheet says why.

## Findings

### 1. The quarry speed ladder is a dead row at every depth, and its gain line is fiction
**BLOCKER.** `cellMs()` in `src/quarry.js` is `Math.max(60, per * own / …)` with `per = CUT_DIG_MS / cells`; at 475–754 cells `per` is 21–34 ms, so the floor of 60 ms binds at pace 0 and no rung can move it. The time actually goes to walking between cells at `CUT_STEP` (one quarrier spends 3184 of 3600 frames walking).
```
benches 2 pace 0 cells 475 cellMs 60.0 | pace 9 cellMs 60.0
benches 5 pace 0 cells 754 cellMs 60.0 | pace 9 cellMs 60.0
digcycle 600s: benches=2 pace 0 -> 14 shards (1.40/min); pace 9 -> 14 shards (1.40/min)
digcycle 600s: benches=5 pace 0 -> 45 shards (4.50/min); pace 9 -> 45 shards (4.50/min)
```
The row reads `+16% shards/min` from `quarryRate()` (`60000/quarryMs`), a function nothing but the gain line uses: 5.45 → 53 trips/min over twelve rungs, against a real output that does not change by one shard. Band four of this ladder (`labcave`) bills 7 cores + 12 sparks for it.
